Music player for Linux handhelds

Your music keeps playing while you game.

audiofilus is a pixel-art music player with album covers, visualizers, eight arcade games and a chiptune tracker. Close it, open an emulator, and the music goes on. Turn the screen off and control it from your pocket.

Background play

A player that stays out of the way

The audio lives in its own service

The interface is just a remote. Close it and the music keeps going; launch a game and it still plays, with gapless track changes and a quick fade when you pause.

Controls from inside any game

Hold FN and tap a button to pause, skip, go back or seek. Your headphones' play button works too, over Bluetooth or cable: one press pauses, two skip, three go back.

Sleep mode for your pocket

Press START twice or the power button: the screen turns off, the CPU slows down and the triggers become gestures. Close the lid and it stays asleep. On an RG34XX-SP it draws about 260 mA in this mode: around 11 to 12 hours of music on its 3300 mAh battery.

Help is one button away

Hold FN on any screen to see what every button does there.

The controls guide, shown while FN is held.

START opens the More menu, including “Background” to leave the app and keep listening.

Visualizers and games

Something to look at

Nine visualizers that follow the beat, from pixel scenes that are light on the CPU to MilkDrop with its classic .milk presets. Colors come from the album cover. Any of them can also play dimmed behind the player.

Tracker

Make your own chiptune

An LSDJ style tracker for the D-pad, with a touch of PICO-8: chains and phrases, instruments with envelopes and tables, grooves with swing, editable waves, 21 effect commands and a piano roll. Write for a Game Boy's four channels or go Free with eight synth channels. Import MOD, XM and LSDJ songs, export as WAV, XM or MOD, and play or sync it from a USB MIDI keyboard or drum machine. Exports land in your library, ready to play in the background like anything else. Tracker guide.

The games and the tracker live behind one Games entry in the screens dock, each game with its own record. And there are a few secrets hidden around the app.

Colors

Every album paints it

The interface takes its colors from the cover that's playing. Change the album and the whole player, the visualizers and the games follow.

Shown with four albums from a real library: each cover gives two accents and a tint for the background.

Lyrics

Sing along

Synced lyrics tint word by word as they're sung, with dots counting down the instrumental parts. They come from a .lrc next to the song, from its tags, or from LRCLIB online.

Pick another version from the list, nudge the timing by half a second, or keep it offline. The lyrics in this animation are made up.

Sound

Tune it the way you like

ReplayGain

Every song at the same loudness, or every album, or automatic: album in order, song on shuffle. It never pushes a song past its peak.

Graphic or parametric equalizer

Ten bands with presets for rock, metal, vocals, bass and more, or a parametric one with up to eight bands (peak, shelves, pass filters) and its curve on screen. Flat, it costs nothing.

Crossfade, DSD and CUE sheets

Up to 12 seconds of crossfade that leaves albums gapless. DSD files play, albums ripped to one file with a CUE sheet show up as their tracks, and the output runs at 44.1, 48, 96 or 192 kHz, with a high-quality resampler on the desktop (a light one on handhelds, to save battery).

Stats from everything you've played, counted by the service even with the player closed.

A heart for liked songs, a moon with the minutes left on the sleep timer.

Your listening

It remembers what you play

Liked songs and ratings

Like a song or rate it from one to five. On a Subsonic, Navidrome or Jellyfin server, the hearts and stars sync both ways.

Recent, most played and stats

Lists that build themselves, plus your hours, top artists, albums and songs.

Scrobbling

Sign in to Last.fm with a QR code, or paste your ListenBrainz token. Listens wait while you're offline and go out when you're back, and plays count on your own server too.

Sleep timer

Stop the music after 15 to 90 minutes or at the end of the song, even with the player closed.

Sources

Your files, your server, the radio

Everything shows up in the same library and the same player. Remote songs are cached on the SD card so the next listen is free.

Local

Your library

Folders, tags, embedded and folder covers, M3U playlists and your own playlists. Scanned once, then only new files are read. Synced lyrics light up line by line, from a .lrc, the tags or LRCLIB. Console soundtracks too: NES, SNES, Game Boy, Genesis and more, one track per song. Fix tags and missing covers from MusicBrainz, compute ReplayGain, and let smart playlists fill themselves.

Server

Subsonic

Navidrome and other Subsonic or OpenSubsonic servers. Songs stream, stay cached for the next listen, and can be downloaded. Edit their playlists from the app.

Server

Jellyfin

Sign in with your Jellyfin user and browse its music library and playlists like your own files.

Live

Internet radio

Thousands of stations from radio-browser.info, by country or genre, with the current song title from the stream. Bring your own .m3u or .pls station lists, or add a station by its address. HLS streams play too.

Episodes

Podcasts

Top shows for your country, search, or any RSS feed by address. Episodes download for offline listening and remember where you stopped.

Bring your own

YouTube Music

Copy your own yt-dlp next to the app and search appears: listen without downloading, or save songs into your library with cover and tags.

Scene

Module archives

The Mod Archive, Modland, VGMRips and AMP in one screen: browse what's popular, new or random, search, listen, save to your library or open a module in the tracker. They show up in Search too.

Send what's playing to a speaker or TV on your network (DLNA/UPnP or Chromecast), share your library with DLNA, or let your phone send music to the handheld. How it works.

Screens

Made for a D-pad

SELECT opens a dock with every screen; press it twice to jump back to the last one. A QWERTY on-screen keyboard handles search and playlist names. Interface sounds are optional, in two separate switches for menus and for music actions.

Any screen

From square to ultrawide

The layout adapts to the screen's real width instead of picking from a few fixed shapes, so it fills a 1:1 RGB30, a 3:2 clamshell and a 21:9 monitor.

Install

Through PortMaster

KnullimuOSROCKNIXArkOSdArkOSEmuELEC
  1. Download audiofilus-portmaster.zip from the latest release.
  2. Install it with PortMaster, or unzip it into your ports folder.
  3. Open audiofilus from Ports and pick your music folder.
  4. Optional: put a yt-dlp binary in the app's yt-dlp/ folder to enable YouTube Music.

On muOS you can skip PortMaster: copy audiofilus.muxapp to the ARCHIVE folder of your SD card and install it from the Archive Manager; it shows up under Applications.

On a Linux or Windows PC, see Desktop.

Each system is detected at startup. Lid, power button, battery saver and volume use that system's own tools; on a desktop all of that is skipped.

Build from source

One command

Written in Go without cgo for the handhelds. Codec libraries are loaded at runtime when the system has them.

# with Docker, nothing else to install
git clone https://github.com/juanvillacortac/audiofilus
cd audiofilus
docker build --target dist --output dist .

# or with Go installed
./build.sh

Both leave the desktop and arm64 binaries plus the PortMaster zip in dist/.

Desktop

At home on your PC

The same player runs on Linux and Windows, one small binary. On Linux it fits in like a native app on GNOME, KDE, Hyprland and Omarchy.

In your app launcher

Audiofilus shows up with its icon, and right-clicking it offers play/pause, next and previous. Window rules and docks recognize its window. Files and folders open with it from your file manager, on Linux and Windows.

Media keys and your bar

It speaks MPRIS, so your keyboard's media keys, headphones and the desktop's media widget control it, with the cover and the song title. On Omarchy that works with nothing to set up.

Still plays in the background

Close the window and the music keeps going (a setting, on by default on the desktop); open it again and it picks up where it is, no splash. Quit stops everything.

In your system tray

The audiofilus CD sits in the tray, on Linux and Windows: click it to open the player, middle-click to pause, or open its menu for what's playing, play/pause, next, previous, shuffle and quit.

Any window shape

Stretch it wide or make it tall and narrow: in portrait it switches to a phone-style player with a big cover, still in crisp pixels.

Linux

One line to install

curl -fsSL https://juanvillacortac.github.io/audiofilus/install.sh | sh
  1. Downloads the latest release for x86_64 or aarch64 into ~/.local/share/audiofilus.
  2. Links audiofilus into ~/.local/bin.
  3. Adds Audiofilus to your app launcher. Run it again any time to update; your settings stay.

Or set it up by hand

  1. Download audiofilus-linux-amd64.tar.gz (or -arm64) from the latest release and unpack it anywhere.
  2. In that folder, run ./audiofilus desktop install. It adds the launcher entry and the icon to ~/.local/share.
  3. Open Audiofilus from your app launcher. If you move the folder, run step 2 again.

Uninstall: audiofilus quit; audiofilus desktop uninstall; rm -rf ~/.local/share/audiofilus ~/.local/bin/audiofilus*

Windows 10 or later: download audiofilus-windows-amd64.zip, unzip it anywhere and run audiofilus.exe.

iPhone and iPad

In your pocket, for real

The same pixel player on iOS, made for your fingers: everything is tappable, lists scroll under your thumb with momentum, and the music keeps playing with the screen locked.

Touch first

Tap rows, covers, tabs and buttons; drag lists with your thumb; the button bar has real buttons, and the games have touch controls of their own. In portrait, tabs open from a button.

Lock screen and Control Center

The cover, the song and the controls on the lock screen, in Control Center, on your headphones and in CarPlay. AAC from iTunes and AAC radio play through Apple's own decoder.

Your music, your way

Copy songs into On My iPhone → Audiofilus in the Files app, pick any folder from iCloud Drive, or skip local music and stream from Subsonic, Navidrome or Jellyfin, radio, podcasts and YouTube Music. Songs also open in Audiofilus from Files and other apps.

An unsigned audiofilus-ios-unsigned.ipa comes with every release: install it with Sideloadly or AltStore, which sign it with your own Apple ID. Or add the source https://juanvillacortac.github.io/audiofilus/altstore.json to AltStore, SideStore or LiveContainer: new releases show up there as updates.

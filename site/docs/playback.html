Playing music

Now Playing, the queue, the sleep timer, and how the music keeps going in the background.

Now Playing

  • A or Y pauses and resumes.
  • Tap ←/→ for the previous or next song; hold them to rewind or fast-forward. L1/R1 also change song and L2/R2 seek 10 seconds.
  • X turns shuffle on or off; X twice plays a random song.
  • ↑/↓ scratch the vinyl.
  • B goes to the library, START opens the More menu.

The More menu has the album and artist, Lyrics, Like, Rating, Sleep timer, Add to a playlist, Save queue as playlist, Queue, Shuffle, Repeat (Off, All, One), Turn the screen off, Go to background and Quit.

The queue

The Queue screen shows what plays next. Play next puts a song right after the current one; Add to queue puts it at the end. Playback is gapless, pausing fades out, and the queue and position survive restarts.

Sleep timer

Now Playing → More → Sleep timer. Each A moves to the next choice: 15, 30, 45, 60 or 90 minutes, End of song, or off. A moon with the minutes left shows on the cover. The service keeps the timer, so it works with the player closed.

Background play

The music lives in its own service; the player is just a remote. How it closes decides what happens to the music:

  • Go to background (More menu, or Settings → Exit): the player closes and the music keeps playing, also inside games. The console suspends as usual.
  • Quit: stops everything.
  • Closing it any other way (closing the window, PortMaster's SELECT+START, or a crash) depends on Settings → Keep playing on close: on by default on the desktop, off on handhelds.

Controls from inside a game

Press FN three times in a row, then within 1.5 seconds:

ThenDoes
L1 / R1Previous / next song
L2 / R2Rewind / forward 10 s
SELECTPause / resume

It works always, with the player open or closed, and doesn't clash with the system or the emulators.

Sleep: screen off, music on

Sleep turns the screen off and lowers the CPU clock while the music plays, so the console can ride in your pocket. Enter it with START twice, the power button, closing the lid, Now Playing → More → Turn the screen off, or after a while without touching anything (Settings → Sleep after). While asleep, the triggers become gestures:

GestureDoes
L1/R1 twicePrevious / next song
L2/R2 twiceRewind / forward 10 s
L1+L2 together, twicePause / resume
R1+R2 together, twiceA random song
START or the power buttonWake up (the power button not with the lid closed)

A single tap does nothing, so pocket presses don't count. On devices with a vibration motor, each gesture gives a short buzz. On an RG34XX-SP sleep draws about 260 mA: around 11 to 12 hours of music.

On desktops, sleep and its settings don't apply and are hidden.

Headphone buttons

Bluetooth and wired headphone buttons control the music with the player open or closed. A single-button headset follows the usual convention: one press pauses, two skip to the next song, three go back.

Opening files from your file manager

Files and folders you open with audiofilus play as a temporary queue: folders are expanded with everything inside, and nothing is saved as a playlist (use Save queue as playlist to keep it). If the player is already open, it just switches to the new queue.

  • Linux: right-click → Open with → Audiofilus, for audio files, M3U/PLS lists, CUE sheets, console music and folders (set up by the installer or audiofilus desktop install).
  • Windows: audiofilus registers itself the first time it opens: Open with on every supported file, and Play with Audiofilus on folders. Selecting several files queues them all.
  • iPhone and iPad: “Open in” or “Share” from Files and other apps.

On the desktop

  • Media keys and bars: on Linux audiofilus speaks MPRIS, so media keys and your desktop's media widget control it.
  • System tray (Linux and Windows): click to open the player, middle-click to pause, and a menu with what's playing, play/pause, next, previous, shuffle and quit.

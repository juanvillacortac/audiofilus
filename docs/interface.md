# Interface

The interface (`audiofilus` with no arguments, `cmd/audiofilus/ui.go`; code in `internal/ui`) already works on the PC, connected to the service:

- **It starts the service** if it isn't running (`Client.startDaemon`: the `audiofilus` binary in its own folder, with `daemon -detach`), sends commands in the background, polls the state 4 times per second and renews the `awake` permission every 10 s (it lasts 30). When you close it, the music keeps playing.
- **On first run** (no existing `music_dirs`) it opens a folder browser (`picker.go`): A enters, B goes up, X uses the folder. The choice is saved in `audiofilus.json`, the same file the service reads.
- **The library** is scanned in the background with a progress bar and saved to `data/library.json`; on later runs only new or changed files are read (by size and date).
- **Screens**: **Now Playing**, **Library** (with Search), **Queue**, **Visualizer**, **Blocks**, **Rhythm** and **Settings**; you switch between them with the screen bar (Select, see below).
  - **Library** (`browse.go`): sections Songs, Albums (grid), Artists, Playlists (`.m3u` files in the music folder) and Folders. Each section has a stack of views: opening an artist pushes its albums, an album pushes its track list, a folder pushes its subfolders. A opens or plays from there, X plays shuffled, B or ← go back, and ← on the first view moves focus to the sections.
  - **Context menu** (Y, `menu.go`): play, shuffle, play next (`playnext`), add to queue (`add`), go to album or artist. On Now Playing: go to album or artist, view the queue, shuffle, repeat and turn the screen off.
  - **Queue** (`queue.go`): fetched from the service every second while it's on screen; A jumps to that song.
  - **Settings:** folder, rescan, language, shuffle, repeat, volume, turn the screen off after (never, 15 s… 5 min), 24 h clock and swap A and B. Interface options go under `ui` in `audiofilus.json`.
- **App sleep:** after `ui.sleep_after_seconds` with no input (while playing, only on the playback screen; with no music, on any screen, because the console doesn't suspend while the app is open), when the lid closes, or from the menu, it sends `sleep on` to the service and stops rendering frames. It wakes with Start (the service sees it) or with a key on the PC. Gamepad buttons don't wake it, because in a pocket they get pressed by accident and the gestures belong to the service.
- **Languages:** Spanish and English (`lang.go`), picked from `$LANG` on first run and changeable in Settings. Only languages whose letters exist in both fonts. `N()` gives the singular ("1 canción").
- **Gamepad and keyboard** (`input.go`): Ebitengine's standard gamepad (on Linux, SDL's mapping) and the keyboard: arrows, Enter (A), Esc/Backspace (B), S (X), Space (Y, pause), M (Start, "more" menu), Tab (Select, screens), F1 (FN, guide), Q/E (L1/R1), comma/period (L2/R2). The left stick works as the D-pad everywhere (read per controller and snapped to eight directions like Trancazo: on past 35 % of its travel, off below 25 %, so it doesn't flicker at the edge; system devices that pose as controllers, like a PlayStation pad's motion sensors and touchpad, are left out). The help bar at the bottom shows buttons or keys depending on what was used last; receivers that announce themselves as a joystick without a standard mapping don't count as a gamepad.
- **Mouse and touch** (`pointer.go`): while drawing, each screen registers its tappable zones (list rows, album covers, library tabs, the "< back" path, Now Playing's controls and progress bar, the screens dock, the More menu's tiles, Settings tiles, the Games list, and the chips of the bottom help bar, which press their button); on the next Update a click or tap does what the topmost zone under it does. A tap on a row or tile selects it and opens it, like on a phone. Lists scroll naturally: the library lists, the album grid, the queue and Settings register a `scrollArea` and keep their own pixel offset (`scroller`), so their content follows the finger pixel by pixel, keeps going with momentum after a flick, and the wheel scrolls them smoothly; tapping a row picks it without the view jumping, and as soon as the selection changes another way (the D-pad) the view follows it again. In games a swipe is a single press in the finger's direction (no steps, no momentum: in Crypt every extra step was an off-beat MISS); in Blocks a sideways drag moves the piece cell by cell under the finger, a swipe down hard-drops, a swipe up holds and tapping the well rotates. Everything else is gestures turned into the usual actions: dragging becomes ↑/↓ (or ←/→) once per step dragged, with momentum after a quick flick; the mouse wheel is ↑/↓; a long press (0.55 s without moving) is START (the More menu); right click is B and middle click pauses; tapping the screen title opens the dock. Injected actions (`Input.inject`) count as just pressed for that frame, and the steps of a drag go out one per frame. `TestPointerTapAndDrag`, `TestScrollerFollowsFinger` and `TestSwipeInGames` check taps, drag steps, momentum, free scrolling and swipes.
- **Long text:** it isn't cut with "...": the last 16 pixels fade into the background in 4 strips of 4 pixels (75, 50, 25 and 0 %) (`drawTextW`, `drawFaded` and the `fadeSrc` shader); a scrolling title fades on the side it continues on. With Bayer dithering, thin letters broke up into stray dots (the author didn't like it).
- **Volume:** a single scale from 0 to 200 %. Up to 100 it's the system volume; above that, audiofilus's boost. Going up, the system volume rises first and then the boost; going down, the reverse. The boost only goes above 100 with the system at 100 % (if the system volume drops from outside, the boost goes back to 100). The service applies it (`volstep`). On Knulli, while the app is open, a wrapper around `knulli-audio` (as Songo did) routes the volume buttons there. The indicator appears on every change (`volume_seq`), whether it comes from the interface (+ and −), the buttons or the CLI.
- **Exiting:** "Go to background" (Y menu and Settings) leaves the music playing (`background`); "Quit" always stops it (`quit`). When the interface closes any other way (closing the window, PortMaster's Select+Start, or the interface dying) it depends on **Settings → Keep playing on close** (`ui.close_to_background`): unset, it's on for the desktop and off on handhelds. The interface keeps an `attach` connection open to the service; with the option on it sends `background` right away and after every attach (attaching resets it; `background off` withdraws it), so when that connection closes the music keeps going. With it off, the service pauses, saves its position and exits. Tested with `kill -9`.
- **Desktop:** the service publishes itself as an MPRIS player (`internal/mpris`): media keys and Omarchy's music widget control it with no setup.
- `-shots <folder> -frames <folder>` also saves 8 s of the playback screen, to make a GIF (`ffmpeg -framerate 20 -i f%04d.png ...`).
- **To do:** per-song durations.

To try it: `./build.sh && dist/audiofilus` (the interface starts the service by running its own binary as `audiofilus daemon -detach`; `go run ./cmd/audiofilus` works too). `-shots <folder> -music <m>` saves screenshots of every screen at 1:1, 4:3, 3:2, 16:10, 16:9, 2:1 and 21:9, and in portrait at 3:4 and 9:16, without the service.

The interface will be a separate process, written in Go with the author's Ebitengine fork (the one from Trancazo, with an SDL2 backend for KMS/DRM and no cgo), which talks to the service over the [socket](protocol.md). This document collects what the author asked for before starting it.

## Aesthetics

- **Retro and pixelated, but modern**, like Songo #5: clean, fast and comfortable, not a crude imitation of the 90s.
- **Albums with their covers**, and eye-catching effects.
- **Kage shaders** as in Trancazo: dithering, scanlines, chromatic aberration, transitions. See what can be reused from `trancazo/game/render/`.

## Screens

It must look good and be **readable** at any aspect ratio: 1:1, 4:3, 16:9, 16:10, 3:2, etc. The target handhelds are 640×480 and 720×480 (RG34XX-SP, 3:2) and 720×720; also the PC in a window.

## Games

The minigames share one **Games** entry in the screens dock (`games.go`): a list on the left (icon and name per row, the chosen one marked like in Settings) and the chosen game large on the right, with what it's about and the record from `data/`; ↑/↓ to choose, A to play, B to go back. "Back to games" in each game's pause menu returns there, and the dock highlights Games while any of them is open.

- **Crypt** (`crypt.go`): a rhythm roguelike. Random floors of rooms joined by corridors (44×30 tiles of 12 px), lit around the player, with stairs in the farthest room. A step (or an attack, stepping into an enemy) only counts within `min(0.17 s, 35 % of the period)` of a beat; off the beat it's a MISS. The game keeps its own beat grid, which follows the one from `meter.go` smoothly (tempo and phase a little each frame; the tempo at once only when it really changes, like a new song), and it remembers beats by their time, not their number: the meter's numbering restarts when it re-anchors. Enemies act when each beat's window closes, after the player: slimes bounce every two beats, bats flutter at random every beat, skeletons raise their arms one beat and chase the next (armored ones, from depth 3, take two hits). On-beat steps in a row raise the multiplier (×2 every 8, FEVER ×4 at 24); a MISS or a hit taken resets it, standing still doesn't. Kills drop coins; hearts heal up to five. The floor flashes in a checker on each beat with the cover's colors (two accents in FEVER), and behind the dungeon a faint sigil listens to the music: a ring and a seven-pointed star that turn and throb with the bass, twelve runes lit by the spectrum bands, and rising embers. The rhythm bar shows the next beats closing in on a heart, the hit window around it, "GOOD" or "MISS" after each step; the heart and the floor beat on the game's own clock, at the same moment the marks arrive. With the music paused the game freezes; without a tempo yet it uses a 110 BPM metronome. Record in `data/cripta.json`. `cryptbot.go` plays it for the screenshots and the website animation.
- **Tracker** (`trackerui.go`, engine in `internal/tracker`): chiptune songs on four channels like a Game Boy tracker: two pulses (12.5/25/50/75 % duty), a 16-step triangle and a 15-bit LFSR noise. A song is an order of patterns (up to 64 each) and a tempo; a pattern is 16 sixteenth-note steps per channel, each with a note (C-0..B-7 or OFF), a volume (0-F) and an effect: `Axy` arpeggio (+x, +y semitones at 60 Hz), `Dn` pulse duty, `Bxx` pitch bend (01-7F up, 81-FF down). Pattern view: the D-pad moves; A held plus ↑↓ changes by one and ←→ by a lot (an octave for notes); A alone puts the last value used; B+A deletes; L1/R1 change pattern (R1 at the end makes a new one). Song view: the tempo and the order; A held plus ↑↓ changes a position's pattern, A alone opens it, A on the empty slot at the end appends. X switches views, Y plays or stops (the pattern looping, or the song from the chosen position) and pauses the music. Start: play the whole song, **export it as WAV to the music folder** (`audiofilus tracker/`, then the library rescans), duplicate or clear the pattern. Notes sound when written (`Player.Preview`). Each channel header has a live oscilloscope (`Player.Scope`, ~21 ms of its wave), cells light up when their note plays, the playing row flashes harder on each beat, and playing the song makes the pattern view follow it. B+Y mutes the channel under the cursor. The synth is an `io.Reader` streamed through the interface's own audio output (`playStream` in `sounds.go`, 50 ms buffer). Songs are saved in `data/tracker/` (one `.json` each, `current` names the open one) a second after each change. Start → **Songs** lists a new empty song, the saved ones and four examples (`internal/tracker/examples.go`: Pocket Pop, Crypt Crawl, Night Drive, Amen Chip); opening an example saves nothing until it's edited (then as `example-*.json`, reopened from that example's entry), and Start → Delete this song removes the open one.
- **Name that song** (`quiz.go`): ten rounds; each loads a random local song through the service, seeks to 0:35 and offers it among four (other titles), in a 2×2 grid. Ten seconds per round; points for the time left, a streak multiplier (×2 from the fourth in a row). Record in `data/adivina.json`. Pauses the music at the end.
- **Snake** (`snake.go`): one step per subdivision of the beat (quarter, eighth or sixteenth, the closest to 1/8 s), from the smooth grid; coins grow it, a heart may appear on downbeats for three beats. Record in `data/serpiente.json`.
- **Ships** (`shooter.go`): the ship fires on every eighth note, three shots in a fan when the beat has bass; enemies arrive with the beats (more as the score grows), shooters fire every two beats. Three lives. Record in `data/naves.json`. `gamebots.go` plays Snake and Ships for the screenshots and website.
- **Runner** (`runner.go`): an auto-runner modeled on Chromium's offline dinosaur. One hit ends the run (A restarts). A or ↑ jumps; holding it gives a full jump (30 half-resolution pixels, exactly one beat long: gravity is derived from the tempo), releasing early cuts it short after a minimum hop, and ↓ in the air drops fast (×3 gravity). The world scrolls a fixed distance per beat, rising from 36 to 56 px with the distance run; the speed eases toward that target, so neither a tempo re-estimate nor a score bonus makes it jump. Each obstacle is planned for a beat (on the current grid) but then lives at a fixed spot in the world (`ruThing.d`), so the scenery only ever scrolls (`TestRunnerScrollsSmoothly` changes the tempo and adds a bonus mid-run); the beat marks on the ground are world things too. They come from the drum **pattern** like Rhythm (`beat.drums.at`): a kick (or cymbal) on a beat brings a speaker (one, two side by side, or a tall amp), a snare a flying vinyl at one of three heights (low: jump it; middle: duck under it, or jump; high: harmless unless you jump into it). At least a beat and a half separates two obstacles (a full jump and its landing). Notes only go where they can be caught: at the apex of the full jump above a speaker, or at ground level on off-beat hi-hats far from any obstacle (`TestRunnerReachable` checks every case against the real hitboxes, `ruHeroBox` and `box`). Jumping on the eighth before an obstacle (the "and") puts the apex on it: PERFECT, raising a multiplier up to ×8. The hero is a 12×12 sprite with headphones and a two-frame run (one step per eighth), a long low duck pose and a crossed-eyes crash frame. Score in five digits with the record ("HI"), blinking at every hundred. Behind, the Plasma visualizer (`plasmaInto`) mapped to the cover's palette, dimmed so the foreground reads, and a horizon line that vibrates with the spectrum; the ground has scrolling pebbles and a mark per beat. Drawn at half resolution and scaled ×2. Record in `data/carrera.json`; `botRunner` plays it for the screenshots and website.
- **Duel** (`duel.go`): turn-based battles. Your turn: a cursor crosses the attack bar in two beats, timed by the beat clock: it leaves on a beat, so its center lands exactly on the next one (the bar marks each beat and eighth); A hits harder near the center (up to 8), ×1.5 on the beat. Their turn, eight beats: a heart dodges, inside a 160×80 box, the notes each foe throws on every beat. Notes move in surges, fast on the beat and slowing until the next one, and the song's drums (`beat.drums`) add more: each snare sends notes aimed at the heart from the top corners, a cymbal a fan from the foe, and the kick shakes the box; the box border pulses on every beat. The patterns: the scratched vinyl, rotating rings; the tangled tape, sweeping lines with a gap; the metronome, columns falling on alternating sides (its pendulum swings with the grid). With bass, one more note aimed at the heart. Beaten foes come back tougher. The foes are 1-bit sprites (one color, details as holes). Record in `data/duelo.json`; `botDuel` plays it for the screenshots and website.
- **Blocks** (`tetris.go`) and **Rhythm** (`rhythm.go`): both use the same smooth beat grid (`steadyBeat` in `meter.go`, with `followGrid`, which Crypt also uses), so Blocks' beat bar doesn't jump when the meter corrects itself, and Rhythm places each new note on the current grid instead of adding half beats one after another (a correction used to leave every later note out of step).

## Easter eggs

`eggs.go`. Spoilers for whoever reads the docs:

- **Konami code** (↑↑↓↓←→←→ B A, anywhere) toggles **Game Boy mode**: the whole frame goes through a shader (`gbSrc`) to the four DMG greens with Bayer dithering, with the boot "ding". Saved as `ui.game_boy`.
- **Special days**: on January 1st, October 18 (audiofilus' first commit) and October 26 (a birthday), confetti or hearts fall for a few seconds after opening the app, with a banner.
- **Scratch**: ↑/↓ on Now Playing jerk the vinyl and, while music plays, scratch the song itself (`scratch` command, `internal/engine/scratch.go`): the engine keeps the last 3 s it generated, flushes the output buffer and plays that stretch back and forth with a hand's speed curve (↑ one long baby scratch, ↓ two short ones). Pitch follows the speed like a real turntable, near standstill it goes silent and slow movement sounds muffled (a one-pole filter that opens with speed); afterwards playback resumes exactly where it was heard, playing first the generated audio the flush threw away. Paused, the synthesized effect sounds instead.
- **Crypt**: every five floors a cracked wall (`crSecret`) hides a 3×3 room with coins, a heart and a rainbow floor; an on-beat hit breaks it.

## Splash, Settings and screen off

- **Closing the lid enters sleep** immediately, with no fade, on any screen (`lidClosed`: Insert on Anbernics with a lid; not on the PC). Opening it doesn't wake: Start does.
- **The sleep timer only counts on the playback screen** (not while browsing or in Settings). The console's volume buttons, which don't go through the interface, also reset the timer (via `VolumeSeq`).
- **Boost with limiter**: above 100 % the system stays at 100 and the service amplifies its signal up to ×2 with a peak limiter (`internal/engine/limiter.go`: instant attack, 80 ms release, -0.2 dB ceiling). This way songs mastered very loud get louder without hard clipping. At 100 % or below the signal passes through untouched.
- **Volume indicator**: slides in from the top only if it wasn't visible; if it already is, the number and the bar change. The service notifies the interface instantly over the `attach` connection (`control.Notify`) when the volume or sleep changes, and applies the system volume in the background (knulli-audio takes ~100 ms per call).
- **Splash** (`internal/ui/splash.go`): "audiofilus" drops letter by letter with a bounce, waves, an LED equalizer pulses and the signature appears (GitHub logo + juanvillacortac). It lasts 2.8 s, any button skips it and the library loads behind it. With `-frames` it's saved to `frames/splash/`.
- **Settings** is a mosaic of tiles grouped by section (Library, Playback, Screen, System, Exit). Toggles: A flips them. Choices: all options show as chips if they fit (otherwise, the value and one dot per option); A opens the choice, left/right pick immediately, A or B close it. Volume: the same, in steps of 10. Actions (folder, rescan, quit): A. The arrows go to the nearest tile in that direction, so the mosaic works with 2 or 3 columns depending on width.
- **Screen off**: entering sleep, the screen dissolves to black with an 8×8 Bayer pattern (0.5 s; touching anything cancels it) and on return it undissolves in 0.25 s (`dissolveSrc` in `shaders.go`; with `-frames` in `frames/sleep/`). The **sleep mode** (`ui.sleep_mode` in `audiofilus.json`) is `backlight` (the service turns off the backlight with `knulli-brightness dispoff`; on the PC it does nothing) or `oled` (it only paints black: `sleep on oled`). In both, idlewatcher stops and gestures work.
- **Headphones in the top bar**: every 2 s, and only when asked for the state, the service checks PulseAudio's default output (`pactl info` and `pactl list sinks`, in `platform/output.go`). A bluez output (or `device.bus = "bluetooth"`) is bluetooth. An active port or an output that mentions headphones (headphone, headset) is wired, unless the port says "not available". The `output` field of the state is `speaker`, `wired`, `bluetooth` or `""`. With the speaker, nothing is drawn. On the RG34XX-SP (H700) PulseAudio doesn't see the jack; there it reads the kernel's "Headphone detection" pin in `/sys/kernel/debug/gpio` (high = plugged in), which Knulli mounts at boot. Verified by plugging and unplugging.
- **Battery in the top bar**: every 5 s the interface reads the first source in `/sys/class/power_supply` of type `Battery`, with its level (`capacity`) and status (`status`). With `Charging` or `Full` a bolt appears next to the battery and the level steps up to full, in a loop (whenever the screen redraws).
- **Album grid**: covers are between 48 and 64 px and are spread to fill the width (`gridLayout`): 4 columns at 1:1, 4:3 and 3:2, and 5 at 16:9.
- **Testing formats with gamescope**: inside gamescope (`GAMESCOPE_WAYLAND_DISPLAY`) the interface starts fullscreen and takes its format, e.g. `gamescope -W 720 -H 480 -- dist/audiofilus` for 3:2. Before, it stayed in a 4:3 window with black bars.
- **Sleep without rendering**: after painting black, the interface switches to `FPSModeVsyncOffMinimum`. Ebitengine stops producing frames: it doesn't call Update or Draw or present anything, and the black stays on screen. It only wakes on an input event or when the client sees that the service left sleep (`Client.OnWake` → `ebiten.ScheduleFrame`). OnWake also sets a flag (`App.woke`) that exits sleep: with frames stopped, the interface never gets to see the service *in* sleep and can't infer from the state change that it woke up (that's how the screen used to stay on and black). On handhelds the keyboard doesn't wake it: on the RG34XX-SP the lid sends Insert/Delete through the same device as the gamepad, and closing it was waking it. In sleep the state is polled every second instead of every 250 ms, and the service doesn't check the audio output. On the PC the interface went from several % CPU to ~0.2 %. On the console it still needs measuring: the fork's SDL2 backend keeps polling events at 60 Hz in that mode.
- **Progress bar with transition**: when seeking forward or back, the bar and the time travel the jump in ~0.25 s with easing (`tweenPosition`). The interface assumes the jump happened (`seekBy`) while the service responds. On song change, the bar jumps with no transition.
- **Real visualizer**: the service computes the spectrum of what's playing (1024-sample FFT with a Hann window, 16 logarithmic bands from 40 Hz to 16 kHz, `engine/meter.go`), the level and the bass, every ~21 ms, along with the time it will be heard (now plus two audio buffers: the player's and oto's context's). Only with the app open and awake. The interface receives it with the state and draws it on time (`ui/meter.go`); each bar highlights what changes relative to the last second (modern mixes sit near the top almost all the time), rises instantly, falls slowly and leaves a peak marker.
- **The background reacts to the music** (`bgSrc`, `App.updatePump`): the first light blob grows and brightens with the bass, the second with the treble (they rise fast and fall slowly), and each beat sends out a ripple from the first. On every screen: strongly on Now Playing and Rhythm, and at ~55 % on the rest so it doesn't get in the way of reading. It's still quantized and dithered as always. The beat (`updateBeat`) is tracked once per frame for the whole app (`App.beatHit`).
- **Buttons on every screen** (since October 18, 2026): **Start opens the screen's "more" menu** (the one for what's playing, for the selection in the library or the results, the pause menu in the games, the visualizer list), **Y pauses or resumes** (it used to be the other way round), L1/R1 change song and L2/R2 seek back or forward.
- **Screen bar** (`dock.go`): Select opens a row of icons (Now Playing, Library, Search, Queue, Visualizer, Downloads —only with yt-dlp—, Blocks, Rhythm, Settings) that slides up from the bottom, with the selected one's name above it and a dot under the current one. When it opens it's already on the **previous screen**, so pressing Select twice switches between the last two, like Alt+Tab; ←/→ choose, Select or A go, and B or Start close. **B returns** to the previous screen in Queue, Settings, Visualizer and Downloads (on Now Playing, B still goes to the library). It replaces cycling through the screens in a row with Select, double Select, and holding Select (they weren't practical with seven screens).
- **Controls guide** (`guide.go`): while **FN** is held (F1 on the keyboard) it shows, in two columns, what each button does on that screen and on all screens, and how to use sleep (its gestures) and background mode (the FN ×3 shortcut); releasing it closes it.
- **Double Start: sleep.** The first Start opens the "more" menu; if the second comes within 0.35 s, the menu closes and the screen turns off (`doubleStart`, in `menu.go`).
- **Logo** (`logo.go`): 2000s media player look, without copying Winamp's lightning bolt or flame: a shiny CD (chrome rim, grooves, spindle and a rotating glint) and "audiofilus" in chrome letters, pixel-bold and pixel-italic, with outline, bevel, a sweeping shine, star sparkles and a curved underline. The CD is drawn pixel by pixel (`WritePixels`) and the letters come from a mask: bevel and outline are the shifted mask, and the chrome is the mask row by row with each row's color. It's used by the splash and the PortMaster cover (which puts the logo on top and the vinyl and tagline below). While FN is held no other button is handled, because Knulli uses FN+button for its shortcuts. In the button pills (the bottom help bar and the guide), arrows are drawn as chevrons (`hint_l`, `hint_r`, `hint_u`, `hint_d`), not with the font's "<" and ">".
- **Transition** (`transition.go`): on screen change, the new one slides in from one side and pushes the old one out (0.16 s, with easing, in whole pixels, with an accent stripe on its edge): from the right if it comes later in the screen bar, from the left if it comes earlier. (There was a dithered curtain; it was slow and nobody liked it.)
- **Rhythm** (`rhythm.go`): a lane game. Notes fall down four lanes, one per drum instrument (kick, snare, hi-hat, cymbals, on Y B X A), and reach the line on the tempo grid. Since they have to appear 1.3 s before they sound and the meter only sees half a second ahead, they come from the drum **pattern**: which eighth note of the bar each instrument hit in the last two bars (`beat.drums`). Music repeats its patterns, so the notes follow the song and change with it (before there's a pattern, one note per beat). Each instrument is recognized by how much its bands rise, with its own threshold; a treble hit much louder than the usual hi-hat is a cymbal. Minimalist look: thin lines, flat notes and each lane's button on its receptor (the arrow if the keyboard is in use). One note at a time (priority kick, snare, cymbal, hi-hat), two-handed with neighboring buttons: ← ↓ on the D-pad and B A (↑ → Y X as an alternative; D F J K on the keyboard) —with the whole diamond you had to jump between opposites—; windows of ±50/100/150 ms (PERFECT, GREAT, GOOD); a combo that multiplies, FEVER at 50, accuracy, max combo and high score in `data/ritmo.json`. Start pauses (resume, pause the music, new game, quit), Select changes screen, L1/R1 change song. Hits are felt: the receptor flashes white and then the lane's color, a beam shoots up the lane, a square ring grows and, on PERFECT, sparks fly; the line lights up and the judgment appears big, with a bounce and its color, with the combo below. Pressing with no note only highlights the receptor's edge; a missed note dims it and makes it shake. With no tempo yet, "Listening for the beat...".
- **Visualizer** (`visual.go`): one screen with eight visualizers (←/→ switch, A camera or variant, Start the list to pick from, Y pause, B back). Each one draws in intensity and `paletteSrc` maps it onto the palette ramp (background → accent → text) with dithering: a single color. Drift and Trench at full resolution; the rest at half (chunky pixels).
  - **Drift** (`drift.go`): an original low-poly coupe (cabin, pop-up headlights, full-width taillight, rims, wheel arches; no brands) drifts along a road made of music: a new curve every bar, tighter the louder it plays; speed follows the tempo, speeding up and slowing down gradually; streetlights that flash; the city in the background is the spectrum and the sun pulses with the bass. It dances: bounces with the kick, sways with the snare, jumps with the cymbals; smoke when drifting. Three cameras.
  - **Bars** (Winamp), **Tunnel** (feedback in the style of MilkDrop/WMP's Battery), **Plasma** (WMP's Ambience), **Waves** (oscilloscope with phosphor). The waves are synthesized from the spectrum (the service doesn't send the waveform).
  - **Trench** (`trench.go`): an original fighter (delta wing, two engines) flies through the trench of a station whose modules come from the spectrum; it zigzags, banks with the snare, barrel-rolls with the cymbals, engines that pulse with the bass, shots on each beat from the cannons under the wings, turrets that shoot at it.
  - **Ridges** (`ridges.go`): the stacked lines of the *Unknown Pleasures* cover. Every 1/12 s a new line holds the spectrum of that moment plus its own noise; lines enter at the bottom and rise, and each one blacks out what's below its curve, so it hides the ones behind (all of it in one `rectBatch`: a fill and a line per column). Lines that entered on a beat glow brighter. Variant 0: bass in the center, in peaks, like the pulsar; variant 1: bass to treble, left to right.
  - Own 3D engine (`r3d` in `drift.go`): projection, flat shading with a height gradient on the car and the ship, near-plane clipping, layered painter's algorithm, a single `DrawTriangles`.
- **The vinyl follows seeks**: when seeking forward or back it spins in that direction as much as it would spin playing those seconds.
- **Blocks** (`tetris.go`): one more screen in the Select rotation. Switching screens keeps the game. Mechanics of modern games, like Apotris: SRS rotation with its wall kicks, DAS/ARR (10 and 2 frames), soft drop of one row every 2 frames, 0.5 s lock delay with 15 move resets (reaching lower restores them), hold (X, once per piece), three next pieces visible, two hidden rows at the top, gravity following the standard curve, T-spins (three-corner rule) and back-to-back (×1.5). Music beats no longer drop the piece (it broke the flow); lines clear on the beat if it comes within 0.25 s, otherwise right away.
- **The beat** (`meter.go`, `beat`): beats from spectral flux (how much the bass and mid bands rise relative to the previous point, with an adaptive threshold), tempo from the median of the intervals folded into 70–180 BPM (it changes only if the new one is confirmed) and a beat grid that keeps going even if the music drops; detected beats correct its phase. All on real time (not frame time). It resets on song change. Flat pieces with clipped corners, in the theme's colors. It reacts to the music: on each bass beat the piece drops a row, bounces a pixel, the well hops slightly and its border flashes; behind the pieces faint columns rise with the spectrum, and stacked pieces light up by column; the tempo is computed (BPM, median of the intervals between beats) and dropping a piece within ±0.1 s of a beat is worth double ("ON BEAT!"); clearing lines makes the background pump. It's a rhythm game: a **lane** in the panel shows the upcoming beats (from the tempo) approaching a line; dropping on time raises the **combo** (×2, ×3…, multiplies the points) and off-beat breaks it; at ×4 you enter **FEVER** (double points, a border alternating the accents, a more intense background). Full lines blink and **explode on the next beat**, with a horizontal ripple in the background; when a piece is dropped, a **laser** crosses the well where it landed. The background is a rhythm shader (`rhythmSrc`): square rings coming out of the center on each beat, flash, scanlines and the usual dithering. In the mockup (`-shots`, `-frames`) the meter is made up, at 120 BPM. Controls: arrows, ↓ fast, ↑ drop, A rotates right and B left, X holds, Start opens the pause menu (resume, new game, quit), Select opens the screen bar; the music, without leaving: Y pause, L1/R1 song, L2/R2 seek. The high score goes in `data/bloques.json`. It isn't named after the original game, which is a trademark.
- **Juice on the playback screen** (`juice.go`): on song change the cover slides in from the side (next: right; previous: left) with a bounce and a stepped flash, and the vinyl gets a burst of speed (scratch); pausing or resuming makes the button "pop" with a growing ring, and the previous or next button does the same on song change; seeking forward or back nudges the bar in that direction. Pixel confetti was tried and removed (nobody liked it). With `-frames` there's a sample of all three.
- **Menu (Y)** (`menu.go`): a panel that slides up from the bottom with a short bounce. On top, the cover, title and artist of the selection; below, a mosaic of icon buttons (3 columns on narrow screens, 4 on wide ones) navigated with the arrows, like Settings. Toggle buttons (Shuffle, Repeat) light up when active, show their value and don't close the menu; Quit has a different color. The bottom help bar switches to the menu's.
- **Changing song without triggers**: on the playback screen, a tap of left/right goes to the previous/next song and holding it seeks back/forward (10 s every 0.2 s, after 0.3 s held; `tapOrHold`). L1/R1 still change song. That way everything works on consoles without triggers.
- **User playlists** (`playlists.go`): Start on a song, album, artist, folder or result → **Add to a playlist** (a new one, named with the on-screen keyboard, or an existing one; duplicates aren't added). In the player, also **Save queue as playlist**. Inside a playlist, Start on a song: move up, move down or remove; Start on a playlist: rename, new or delete (asks first). With the playlists tab empty, Start creates one. New ones go in `data/playlists/` as `.m3u8` (without touching the music folder); `.m3u` files in the music folder can be edited too. When saving, anything inside the playlist's folder gets a relative path (so the playlist still works on the PC) and the rest an absolute one. The name is typed with the on-screen keyboard (uppercase only: it's saved in "Title Case") or with the PC's (as is); characters not valid on exFAT are replaced with `_`.
- **YouTube Music** (`internal/ytm`, `ytmusic.go`): only if the user puts **their own yt-dlp** next to audiofilus: the unpacked version in `yt-dlp/` (the `yt-dlp_linux_aarch64.zip` zip extracted there; it starts in ~3 s on the console, the single-file one in ~11), a single `yt-dlp` file, or one on the PATH. audiofilus doesn't ship it. With it, Search has filters (**All**, **Library**, **YouTube**; L1/R1) and when you finish typing it also searches YouTube Music: the search is the web API with no account, in pure Go (~1–2 s), filtered to songs. Its results go below, with the YouTube icon in red, and with a mark if it's probably already in the library: its `youtube_id` in `.library.json` (from ytm-gym or from here) or the same first artist and title (without what's in parentheses, brackets or after " - ", without accents or punctuation; `songKey`). A or Start open its menu: **Listen** (yt-dlp downloads the AAC to `/tmp/audiofilus-ytm`, with the tags in `<id>.m4a.tags.json` because YouTube audio has none, and the thumbnail at 544×544 in `<id>.m4a.jpg`, and plays it; the last 15 are kept. While it plays, the player builds a "passing" album with that cover: you see the cover, the vinyl and the colors as with any song, without the links to album and artist, which aren't in the library; with the YouTube icon in the corner of the cover and "STREAMING" next to the status), **Download to library** (if the song is already in the listen cache and ffmpeg is present, that file is converted with its thumbnail and tags, without going back to YouTube: only the conversion step; otherwise, yt-dlp. In both cases, like ytm-gym: `Artista/Álbum|Singles/Título.mp3` at 192 kbps, square cover, clean tags and `TXXX:youtube_id`, and it's recorded in `.library.json`; without ffmpeg, the `.m4a` as is; when it finishes, the library updates itself) and, if it's already there, **Play the one you have**. When YouTube asks to sign in, two cases are told apart (`loginKind`): **age-restricted or members-only** videos (`ErrLogin`): audiofilus searches YouTube with yt-dlp for another upload of the same song (same title, duration within 15 %: the official video, a lyric video...) and uses its audio with the chosen song's tags and cover; and **"confirm you're not a bot"** (`ErrBot`), which is about the network (too many requests from that IP) and passes in hours, so no other upload is tried. For both, the user can put a `cookies.txt` exported from their browser (Netscape format) in the `yt-dlp/` folder or next to audiofilus; it's passed with `--cookies` (and the web client is tried first, since it uses it). The messages say which case it is and what to do. A failed search (no network) is silent: Search just shows the library. yt-dlp is always called with `--force-ipv4` (the URL is tied to the IP; over IPv6 it gave 403 or downloaded slowly) and tries the `visionos` client and the default one. On the console, listening takes ~15 s while awake (almost all of it is yt-dlp extracting the URL; downloading 4 MB, 2 s) and downloading also takes however long ffmpeg needs to convert to MP3 (in sleep, at 480 MHz, 31 s and 78 s). That's why each one is a **job** with a status: yt-dlp prints one progress line per step (`--progress-template`) and from that come "finding the audio", "downloading N %" and "converting". A **bar above the bottom help bar**, on any screen, says what's being done and how far along it is (first the listen, otherwise the current download, with how many more are waiting); at the top, an arrow counts pending downloads; the **Downloads** screen (in the Select bar, only with yt-dlp; also from the menu of a Search result or of the player) lists only downloads, and at the top what's streaming if it hasn't been saved yet ("playing now": A saves it; in the player, Start → Download to library does the same) (what's being listened to without saving only shows in the bar while it loads), from newest to oldest with their status and bar: A plays the downloaded one or retries the failed one. Listening is separate and immediate (requesting another cancels the previous one); downloads are queued, one at a time. The song being listened to stays in the cache: if the wifi drops halfway, it keeps playing. YouTube audio is fragmented MP4: `decode/mp4.go` reads it from the `moof` boxes. `audiofilus ytm search|fetch|download <text>` tests it from the console.
- **Subsonic/OpenSubsonic and Jellyfin servers** (`internal/remote`, `servers.go`): Navidrome, gonic, Airsonic-Advanced, Ampache, LMS, Nextcloud Music... and **Jellyfin/Emby** (`remote/jellyfin.go`: session with `AuthenticateByName` and the token in `ApiKey` —the old `api_key` gives 401 on Jellyfin 12—, catalog from `Items` of type Audio 500 at a time, listening via `/Audio/{id}/universal` transcoded to MP3, downloading via `/Items/{id}/Download`, covers via `Images/Primary`; the interface and the service each use their own device id, because Jellyfin closes the previous session of the same device). Both are a `remote.Backend` and work the same in the interface. They're added in Settings → Servers (first the type, with the usual port pre-filled; then name, address, user and password with the on-screen keyboard; the connection is tested before saving, and if it doesn't respond you can retry, fix it or save anyway) or by hand in `audiofilus.json` (`servers`). Each server is **one more library**, with its own slot in the Select bar (server icon, after the library): albums, artists, songs, playlists and search, built the same way as the local one (`buildLibrary`) with songs at path `subsonic://<server>/<id>` and covers from the server (`getCoverArt`, saved in `data/cache/<server>/covers`). The catalog comes from an empty `search3`, 500 at a time (on the PC, 599 songs in 47 ms), is saved in `data/cache/<server>/catalog.json` (it shows instantly on startup, also with no network) and writes `meta.json`, the tag index the service uses to report what's playing. Per server: test connection, **listening quality** (MP3 at 192 kbps by default —little to download and the cheapest to decode—, 320, 128, 96 or original), refresh catalog, edit details and remove. A server's songs can be played, queued, shuffled and **downloaded to the library** (the original file, with its tags and cover, in `Artista/Álbum/NN Título.ext`, through the same queue and Downloads screen); the server's playlists can be played but not edited from here, and "Add to a playlist" (which is for the card) doesn't show on them.
- **How remote audio plays** (`engine`): the service can't wait on the network in the audio thread, so if the song isn't in the cache yet (`data/cache/<server>/songs`, 400 MB by default, `cache_mb`), silence plays while it downloads (checked every 200 ms) and it starts on its own when it arrives; the state carries `loading` and the interface shows it in the bottom bar ("fetching from the server"). When each song starts, the next one in the queue is downloaded (`Queue.Peek`), so moving on doesn't wait. If it fails, it's skipped. The cache lives on the card and not in `/tmp` because on 512 MB devices `/tmp` is RAM.
- **Long menus**: if not all rows fit (square screens, the country menu), the ones that fit are shown, they scroll with the selection, and a small bar on the right indicates there's more.
- **Layered on-screen keyboard** (`playlists.go`, `openPrompt`): for everything that gets typed (playlist names, servers). QWERTY, like Search's; "aA" toggles upper and lower case, "#+=" switches to symbols and accents, and a fixed row has `. / : @ - ~ ? =` for addresses. Playlist names start in uppercase and are saved in "Title Case" unless lowercase or symbols are used; addresses and usernames start in lowercase and are kept as is; the password is shown as asterisks.
- **Internet radio** (`internal/radio`, `radio.go`): a screen in the Select bar with Favorites, Popular (the most listened in the country —taken from the time zone: `TZ`, `/etc/localtime` or Knulli's `system.timezone`; otherwise from `LANG`— or worldwide with X), Search (by name, with the on-screen keyboard) and Genres. The directory is **radio-browser.info** (free, no key; several mirrors are tried and each listen is reported, as it requests); only MP3 and AAC stations are shown, which is nearly all of them. A listens (the queue is the list you were in: L1/R1 change station), Start: favorite and more (`data/radio.json`). A station travels as `radio://<uuid>`; its data goes to `data/cache/radio/stations.json` so the service knows where to connect. In the service each station is a `decode.Source`: a goroutine reads from the network into a buffer (also from old Shoutcast servers that answer "ICY 200 OK"), extracts the title of what's playing from the ICY metadata and reconnects if it drops; the audio thread decodes from that buffer with the "feed" mode of libmpg123 or libfdk-aac (`decode/stream.go`) without ever waiting: if data is missing, silence until there's ~1.5 s of margin (and the bottom bar says "connecting to the station"). Start → Country picks where the popular ones come from (automatic, worldwide, or one of 14 countries; `ui.radio_country`). With no duration, the progress bar is "live" (how long you've been listening, a line with a pulse running along it and "● LIVE"; also in the mini player) and you can't seek. In the player: the station's logo (downloaded from the directory, if it's JPEG or PNG), the title the station sends and "LIVE".
- **Podcasts** (`internal/podcast`, `podcasts.go`): a screen in the Select bar with Subscribed, Popular (the Apple Podcasts chart for the radio's country: `itunes.apple.com/<country>/rss/toppodcasts` and `lookup` for the RSS) and Search (Apple's search, no key); Start in Subscribed adds a podcast by its RSS address. A on a podcast opens its episodes (from its RSS, newest to oldest), with date, duration and status: played, "at 12:34" or downloaded. Episodes are **downloaded** to `data/podcasts/<podcast>/<date> <title>.ext` (with the podcast's cover and the tags alongside, for the player) and play from the card: listening to one that isn't there downloads it (through the Downloads queue) and plays it when done. Your position in each one is remembered (`data/podcast-progress.json`, saved every 15 s) and playback resumes from there (3 s earlier, to get your bearings); near the end it's marked as played. Start on an episode: listen, download or delete the download, mark as played or unplayed. Start on a podcast: subscribe or remove it, refresh and **Download new ones** (off by default: when refreshing it downloads the most recent episode if it's new). Subscriptions (`data/podcasts.json`) are refreshed on entering if more than an hour has passed. In the player, "PODCAST" with the microphone.
- **Pause with fade** (`engine`): on the player's volume and in real time: on pause it drops to zero in 0.3 s and, once whatever was left in the output buffer (250 ms) has been heard, it stops and lets the device sleep; on resume it rises in 0.15 s from wherever it is (if you resume halfway through the fade-out, it rises again from there). Each fade cancels the previous one. (A first version faded the audio while generating it; since it plays 250 ms later, the fade-out stayed in the buffer and on resume it played at full volume, dropped and rose again.)
- **Sounds** (`internal/sfx`, `sounds.go`): short chiptune-style beeps, synthesized in code (no audio files): move, accept, back, open and close menus and the bar, change screen, type, and the music ones (pause, resume, next, previous, ±10 s, shuffle on/off, a random one). At most one plays per frame, the most important one (A on the player sounds like pause, not also like "accept"); in the games, with no menu, buttons make no sound. The interface has its own audio output with a ~50 ms buffer (the service's has 250, the delay would be noticeable), which lives in a separate goroutine (`soundLoop`): the interface only passes it requests and never waits on audio. It's prepared at startup, in the background, and suspended after 2 s without sounds. (Before, it was created with the first sound from the interface thread, and PulseAudio took 7.4 s on the console: with interface sounds off, the first one was the pause sound and the interface froze.) FN shortcuts and sleep gestures, which don't go through the interface, are played by the service over the music (if it was paused, it turns the output on for the sound and stops it again). The games' sounds (Crypt's `cr_*`, used by every game) are a separate kit (`kit` in `internal/sfx`): layered sines with falling pitch (dull thumps), one-pole filtered noise (hits and brushes) and short sine notes with an exponential decay, quieter than the beeps, so they sit under any music (the old square-wave beeps clashed with distorted guitars); `go run ./tools/sfxdemo out.wav` renders them all in a row to listen while tuning. They're two options in Settings, each with its own volume (off, low, medium —default— or high): **Interface sounds** (`ui.ui_sounds`: moving, accepting, menus, screens, typing and the splash) and **Music sounds** (`ui.sounds`: pause, next, previous, shuffle and random, also those of the FN shortcut and the sleep gestures, which the service plays).
- **Splash jingle** (`sfx.Splash`): synced with the logo; a rising sweep when the CD appears, one note per letter as it hits the floor on its first bounce (C, D, E, G, A... going up), a high shimmer when the shine crosses it and a C major chord at the end (2.6 s). If the splash is skipped, it cuts off. It uses the volume from Settings → Sounds.
- **12 h clock** with AM/PM.
- **Double X (shuffle)**: on the playback screen and in the library, two presses of X within 0.4 s play a random song from the whole library, with the rest after it, and announce its title. The first press does its usual thing (toggle shuffle, or play the selection shuffled); the second undoes it and leaves shuffle as it was.
- **Search** (`search.go`): the first tab of the library (you enter it from Songs). With the gamepad, a 4×10 QWERTY on-screen keyboard —numbers on top, Ñ next to L, and space, delete and OK next to M— (A types, B deletes —with no letters, returns to the sections—, X space, Y clears, Start or OK to the results); with the keyboard, you type directly (Enter to the results, Esc to the sections) and while typing keys aren't shortcuts. Live results: artists, albums and songs, case- and accent-insensitive, first those that start with the query, then those with a word starting with it, and last those that contain it (200 max). In the results, A opens the artist or album or plays the song with the rest of its album after it, Start opens its menu, and B (or up from the first one) goes back to typing.
- **Jump by letter**: in the library, L and R go to the start of the previous or next letter, wrapping around at the end (`letters.go`). The letter shows briefly in large type in the center. The letter follows each list's sort order: artist in Songs and Albums, title in an artist's albums, name in Artists and Playlists, and file name in Folders. It's grouped in uppercase and without accents, with numbers and symbols under "#". Inside an album or a playlist, which have their own order, it does nothing. On the playback screen, L and R are still previous and next song.

## The Trancazo recipe (starting point)

Trancazo already solves this with the same fork; it's worth copying its approach (see `trancazo/docs/dibujo.md` and `trancazo/game/render/`):

- **Scaling with hard pixels** (`App.DrawFinalScreen`, `ebiten.FilterPixelated`): fullscreen or in a window of any size the scale isn't an integer, and the default filter smoothed everything; with this one, pixels look hard and the same size at any scale, without the black bars that limiting to integer scales would leave.
- **The short side is always 240 px, and the orientation decides the rest** (`App.Layout` with `ViewSize`), always pixel art scaled without smoothing. In portrait (a phone, a narrow window) the canvas is 240 wide and as tall as the proportion asks, up to 560 (9:16 → 240×427, 3:4 → 240×320): Now Playing grows the cover to fill the width (with the vinyl peeking out) and adds the album under the artist; lists, Settings and menus simply show more; Blocks, Snake and Duel center in the height; the controls guide goes to one column when it fits; the splash logo drops to ×1 where ×2 doesn't fit. In landscape, **a fixed logical height of 240 px and the width from the window** (`ViewW`, like `world.AspectFor` in Trancazo): any width between 240 (1:1) and 560 (21:9), not just fixed formats; anything wider than 21:9 stays at that with bars. For example, 1:1 → 240, 4:3 → 320, 3:2 → 360, 16:10 → 384, 16:9 → 427, 2:1 → 480, 21:9 → 560. 640×480 is 320×240 ×2, 720×480 is 360×240 ×2 and 720×720 is 240×240 ×3: integer scaling on all three handhelds.
- **The essentials inside the central 240×240 square**, which is visible in every format; the extra space on the sides is for decoration or wider lists.
- **Final pass** (`render.DrawFinal`, `finalShaderSrc`): "sharp bilinear" (nearest neighbor at integer scale, blending only at the edges otherwise), and an option for integer scaling with black borders.
- **Pixel fonts at their native size**, never scaled with `GeoM`: Jersey 10 for the interface, Tiny5 at 8 px for long text; `crispFace` leaves each pixel fully on or off. They cover accents, ñ, ¡ and ¿.
- **Reusable shaders:** `limitSrc` (4×4 Bayer dithering and color modes), `tv()` (scanlines and vignette), `avShaderSrc` (chroma aberration), `menuBgShaderSrc` and `sunburstShaderSrc` (animated backgrounds), `pageTurnShaderSrc` (transition), `gaugeShaderSrc` (progress and volume bars), `pixelZoomShaderSrc` (mosaic). Everything quantized with Bayer so gradients come out dithered rather than banded.
- **Mali rules:** avoid per-pixel `sin`/`cos`/`atan2`, reuse `Unmanaged` canvases (`canvas.go`) and warm up shaders and canvases on the loading screen (`Warmup`). Every new shader gets a compile test.

For power saving: outside pocket mode, animated effects cost CPU and GPU every frame; at rest (nothing changing on screen) the interface should stop redrawing.

## Memory: it must work with 512 MB

The RG34XX-SP has 2 GB, but audiofilus must work on 512 MB handhelds, alongside the system, the frontend and the audio service.

- **Original covers aren't kept.** A decoded 1000×1000 cover is 4 MB; keeping them pushed the mockup to 926 MB after scrolling through 408 albums. Now each cover is decoded, downscaled to the size it's drawn at, and the original is released (`Album.decodeCover`). Only its two colors remain.
- **Downscaled covers have a limit** (`maxCovers`, 192, LRU): the ones unseen the longest are freed from the GPU.
- **`debug.SetMemoryLimit(96 MB)`** in the interface, so the garbage collector returns memory sooner.
- **Benchmarking a visualizer:** `dist/audiofilus -music <folder> -bench lake [-benchprof f.prof]` (or `drift`, `trench`, `bars`...) draws 600 frames without the service, with the mockup's beat (a third with each camera, two 1/60 steps per draw, as at 30 fps), and reports how long the CPU takes per frame, how much memory it allocates and how many triangles it produces; `-benchprof` saves the CPU and memory profiles (`f.prof.mem`). On the PC (October 18, 2026): Lake went from 2.56 ms and 136 KB per frame to 1.2 ms and 59 KB (the water height is computed once per mesh point instead of four times, per-ripple work once per frame, and ripples are cut off where they're no longer visible; the engine sorts indices instead of triangles, and `r3d.box` doesn't allocate); Drift from 0.85 to 0.57 ms; Trench ~1 ms. The frames come out pixel-identical.
- **Measuring:** `go run ./cmd/audiofilus -music <folder> -scrolltest` scrolls through all albums and shows memory. On the PC (October 17, 2026, 408 albums): 114 MB at startup, 153 MB peak; the Go heap, 22 MB peak. The rest is the PC's OpenGL driver (Mesa); **it still needs measuring on the console** (Mali with SDL2/KMS).

## Smoothness

- Covers are prepared by two workers (`covers.go`); the interface thread only uploads the ready ones to the GPU, and they appear with a fade. Doing it while drawing caused stutters when switching screens and scrolling.
- The row above and the row below in the grid are requested ahead of time.
- Shaders are warmed up on the first frame (`warmUp`), as in Trancazo.
- Truncations of long titles are cached (`fit`).

## Features: at least those of Songo #5

Taken from Songo's code (see the analysis in the project memory):

- **Library:** all songs, albums, artists, playlists and folders; import at startup; embedded covers and covers fetched online (MusicBrainz, Fanart.tv).
- **Playlists:** `.m3u`, create and edit, add and remove from a quick menu (Y), recent playlist.
- **Playback:** shuffle (X), repeat song, queue ("play next"), follow the current song, previous/next (L1/R1), seeking (L2/R2 and up/down), volume with indicator.
- **Song screen** with a spectrum visualizer and a mini panel while browsing.
- **Sleep:** timer, fade types, button lock, keep the screen on. Here sleep is handled by the service (pocket mode) and the interface **stops drawing entirely** (`SDL_WaitEvent`), instead of just lowering the FPS.
- **Settings:** color themes, interface scale, margins, CRT effect, 24 h clock, battery, network, effects volume, swap A/B and X/Y.
- **About** (in Settings): a tile with the author's GitHub; A opens a panel with its QR code (`qr.go`, with `skip2/go-qrcode`, pure Go), enlarged to a whole number of pixels per module so a phone can read it, and B closes it.

New compared to Songo: exiting while leaving the music playing, and entering pocket mode from anywhere.

# Interface

The interface (`audiofilus` with no arguments, `cmd/audiofilus/ui.go`; code in `internal/ui`) already works on the PC, connected to the service:

- **It starts the service** if it isn't running (`Client.startDaemon`: the `audiofilus` binary in its own folder, with `daemon -detach`), sends commands in the background, polls the state 4 times per second and renews the `awake` permission every 10 s (it lasts 30). When you close it, the music keeps playing.
- **On first run** (no existing `music_dirs`) it opens a folder browser (`picker.go`): A enters, B goes up, X uses the folder. The choice is saved in `audiofilus.json`, the same file the service reads.
- **The library** is scanned in the background with a progress bar and saved to `data/library.json`; on later runs only new or changed files are read (by size and date). With that cache for the same folders, startup shows the library from it at once (`loadCachedLibrary`, no file touched) and the scan runs in the background (`App.refresh`, `pollRefresh`), replacing the library only if something changed (`sameLibrary`): right after power-on, with the SD card on FUSE (exFAT on Knulli), just walking and checking ~600 files took long enough to show the scan screen every time. Only the first scan, or a new folder, shows it.
- **Screens**: **Now Playing**, **Library** (with Search), **Queue**, **Visualizer**, **Blocks**, **Rhythm** and **Settings**; you switch between them with the screen bar (Select, see below).
  - **Library** (`browse.go`): sections Songs, Albums (grid), Artists, Playlists (`.m3u` files in the music folder) and Folders. Each section has a stack of views: opening an artist pushes its albums, an album pushes its track list, a folder pushes its subfolders. A opens or plays from there, X plays shuffled, B or ← go back, and ← on the first view moves focus to the sections.
  - **Context menu** (Y, `menu.go`): play, shuffle, play next (`playnext`), add to queue (`add`), go to album or artist. On Now Playing: go to album or artist, view the queue, shuffle, repeat and turn the screen off.
  - **Queue** (`queue.go`): fetched from the service every second while it's on screen; A jumps to that song.
  - **Settings:** folder, rescan, language, shuffle, repeat, volume, turn the screen off after (never, 15 s… 5 min), 24 h clock and swap A/B and X/Y (Nintendo-style layout, so the hint letters match the console). Interface options go under `ui` in `audiofilus.json`.
  - **Orientation** (Settings → Screen, handhelds only: the UI runs with the SDL2 backend, `handheldUI`): horizontal, or vertical with the console turned left (d-pad at the bottom) or right (d-pad at the top), saved as `ui.orientation` (`""`, `left`, `right`). The canvas is laid out portrait (`ViewSize` with width and height swapped, the same layouts as on a phone) and `DrawFinalScreen` turns it a quarter turn, as large as fits and centered (`rotatedGeoM`). The d-pad, the left stick and A/B/X/Y turn with it (`Input.physical`): each is read from the button that ends up in that position in the hand, so up on screen is up on the d-pad and the bottom button still confirms (the hint letters then name positions, not the printed letters). Shoulders, Start and Select don't turn. Chosen with the d-pad, it turns when the choice closes, so the arrows don't change meaning halfway.
  - **Vertical controls** (next to it, only while vertical): two hands (the default, as above) or one hand, saved as `ui.vertical_controls` (`""`, `one`), for holding the console by its bottom half. With one hand both sticks move (`Input.oneHand`), and with the d-pad at the bottom the d-pad and A/B/X/Y swap jobs, each still read by position: the d-pad's bottom button confirms and the top cluster moves. With the d-pad at the top nothing else changes, since A/B/X/Y and the right stick are already at the bottom.
- **App sleep:** after `ui.sleep_after_seconds` with no input (while playing, only on the playback screen; with no music, on any screen, because the console doesn't suspend while the app is open), when the lid closes, or from the menu, it sends `sleep on` to the service and stops rendering frames. It wakes with Start (the service sees it) or with a key on the PC. Gamepad buttons don't wake it, because in a pocket they get pressed by accident and the gestures belong to the service.
- **Send to…** (`castui.go`, engine in `internal/cast`): Now Playing's START menu discovers DLNA/UPnP renderers and Chromecasts (3 s), casts the current song at the current position with the next queue entry queued, and while casting routes toggle/pause/next/prev/seek/jump/load (a hook at the top of `a.send`) to the session, shows its position and "→ device" in the top bar; "Stop sending" resumes locally. One `cast.Server` per process serves local files by token and transcodes what renderers can't play to WAV.
- **DLNA** (service: `internal/dlna`, `internal/daemon/dlna.go`): Settings → Online toggles *Share library* (MediaServer over the scanned `library.json`) and *Receive music* (MediaRenderer playing through the engine); the UI saves `audiofilus.json` (`dlna`) and sends `dlna apply`. Off by default.
- **Sound, v0.11:** the EQ tile opens a graphic/parametric menu; `peq.go` is the parametric editor (up to 10 RBJ bands, preamp, curve; the curve shrinks to fit ten rows), sending `eqmode`, `peq` (JSON) and `peqpreamp`; the *Resampling* tile sends `resampler auto|linear|quality`. The same menu has **AutoEQ** (`autoequi.go`, `internal/autoeq`): search the AutoEq index for a headphone model (on-screen keyboard; results show who measured it), or pick a `ParametricEQ.txt` from `eq/` next to the app; the profile goes into the parametric EQ, which opens. A profile downloaded from the index is also saved in `eq/` as `<model> ParametricEQ.txt` (`autoeq.FormatParametric`), so it works offline afterwards. **EQ profile** in the Start menu (`eqprofiles.go`, shown once `eq/` has profiles) cycles with A through none and every profile in `eq/`, applying it to the parametric EQ without opening it; the current one is `ui.eq_profile` in `audiofilus.json`. For several headphones, or one with different profiles (ANC on and off). The *Crossfeed* tile (Sound) sends `crossfeed off|light|medium|strong`. See [audio-quality.md](audio-quality.md).
- **Tags and metadata** (`tagsui.go`; `internal/tagwrite`, `internal/musicbrainz`, `internal/loudness`): song/album/artist menus for local files: Edit tags, Album tags, Find cover, Look up on MusicBrainz, Compute/Recompute ReplayGain; writes run as Download jobs and a background rescan picks the changes up.
- **Smart playlists** (`smartui.go`, `internal/smart`): after the automatic lists in Playlists, evaluated when opened; a menu wizard builds rules; stored in `data/smart.json`. The scan cache (v2) stores genre and year for them.
- **Server playlists** (`serverplaylists.go`, `internal/remote/playlists_edit.go`): rename, delete, create, add, remove and move songs on Subsonic/Jellyfin, then the server's playlists are re-fetched.
- **Updates** (`update.go`, engine in `internal/update`): after the splash, at most once a day (`data/update.json`), the UI asks GitHub for the latest release; if it's newer than `version.Version` it flashes a notice once per version. Settings → Update shows the version and state, and A checks or installs: it downloads this installation's package (`update.Detect`: `mux_launch.sh` → muOS app, `port.json` → PortMaster port, else the Linux tar.gz or Windows zip), verifies it against `SHA256SUMS`, writes each file as `.new` and renames it over (on Windows the running `.exe` is renamed to `.old` first, deleted on the next start), then sends `restart` to the service and re-executes itself (`proc.Reexec`). `audiofilus.json` and `data/` are never touched. Not on mobile, nor in `dev` builds. The CLI does the same with `audiofilus update [-check]`.
- **Languages:** Spanish, English and Turkish in `lang.go` (Turkish contributed by volkybaba, with its special letters ç ğ ı İ ö ş ü, which both fonts have), and the rest in `lang_<code>.go` (German, French, Portuguese, Italian, Polish, Russian, Indonesian, Japanese, Chinese, Korean; each registers its map in `init`). Picked from `$LANG` (its first two letters) on first run and changeable in Settings. `TestLanguagesComplete` checks every key exists in every language. `N()` gives the singular ("1 canción"); `decimal()` writes the decimal comma where the language uses one. Japanese, Chinese and Korean (and any CJK in song titles) come from Fusion Pixel 8px, loaded in the background during the splash; Cyrillic and Greek in titles come from Tiny5 at double size, since Jersey 10 lacks them (`fonts.go`).
- **Gamepad and keyboard** (`input.go`): Ebitengine's standard gamepad (on Linux, SDL's mapping) and the keyboard: arrows, Enter (A), Esc/Backspace (B), S (X), Space (Y, pause), M (Start, "more" menu), Tab (Select, screens), F1 (FN, guide), Q/E (L1/R1), comma/period (L2/R2). The left stick works as the D-pad everywhere (read per controller and snapped to eight directions like Trancazo: on past 35 % of its travel, off below 25 %, so it doesn't flicker at the edge; system devices that pose as controllers, like a PlayStation pad's motion sensors and touchpad, are left out). The help bar at the bottom shows buttons or keys depending on what was used last; receivers that announce themselves as a joystick without a standard mapping don't count as a gamepad.
- **No music of your own.** If the music folder is empty or can't be read, the app opens anyway with an empty library (and says so), so servers, radio, podcasts and YouTube Music work without importing anything; Now Playing then suggests adding music or a server or radio. (It used to stay in the folder browser, with no way to reach them.)
- **Touch mode** (`Input.touch`): on with the first touch (and from the start on mobile or with `AUDIOFILUS_TOUCH=1`), off with a key or button. The bottom help bar becomes a row of tappable buttons with their names (no keys or pad buttons; arrow hints are dropped) and grows from 13 to 22 px (`bottomH` is set every frame, so every screen makes room); the mini player gets prev, −10 s, play/pause, +10 s and next buttons and tapping it opens Now Playing; Now Playing's controls get taller touch zones; Rhythm's lanes widen to fit the screen with bigger receptors and no key letters. On narrow screens the library, Radio and Podcasts tabs become a button that opens them in a drop-down (`tabsButton`, `tabDrawer` in `touchui.go`). Radio, Podcasts (shows and episodes), Downloads, search results and the folder browser scroll by finger and open on tap (`touchList`); the search box, its scope chips, the on-screen keyboards, Settings' choice chips and volume bar are tappable too.
- **Mouse and touch** (`pointer.go`): while drawing, each screen registers its tappable zones (list rows, album covers, library tabs, the "< back" path, Now Playing's controls and progress bar, the screens dock, the More menu's tiles, Settings tiles, the Games list, and the chips of the bottom help bar, which press their button); on the next Update a click or tap does what the topmost zone under it does. A tap on a row or tile selects it and opens it, like on a phone. Lists scroll naturally: the library lists, the album grid, the queue and Settings register a `scrollArea` and keep their own pixel offset (`scroller`), so their content follows the finger pixel by pixel, keeps going with momentum after a flick, and the wheel scrolls them smoothly; tapping a row picks it without the view jumping, and as soon as the selection changes another way (the D-pad) the view follows it again. In games a swipe is a single press in the finger's direction (no steps, no momentum: in Crypt every extra step was an off-beat MISS); in Blocks a sideways drag moves the piece cell by cell under the finger, a swipe down hard-drops, a swipe up holds and tapping the well rotates. Games get their own touch controls: Crypt steps toward the side of the hero you touch, on touch (not release: the step goes on the beat; `pressZone`); in Rhythm touching a lane is its button, also on touch; in Ships dragging moves the ship as is and in Duel dragging moves the heart while dodging (`dragArea`), touching anywhere else is A (strike, continue); in Runner touching the upper two thirds jumps (holding it jumps higher) and resting a finger on the lower third ducks (`held`); in Name that song touching a choice answers it. Everything else is gestures turned into the usual actions: dragging becomes ↑/↓ (or ←/→) once per step dragged, with momentum after a quick flick; the mouse wheel is ↑/↓; a long press (0.55 s without moving) is START (the More menu); right click is B and middle click pauses; tapping the screen title opens the dock. Injected actions (`Input.inject`) count as just pressed for that frame, and the steps of a drag go out one per frame. `TestPointerTapAndDrag`, `TestScrollerFollowsFinger` and `TestSwipeInGames` check taps, drag steps, momentum, free scrolling and swipes.
- **Long text:** it isn't cut with "...": the last 16 pixels fade into the background in 4 strips of 4 pixels (75, 50, 25 and 0 %) (`drawTextW`, `drawFaded` and the `fadeSrc` shader); a scrolling title fades on the side it continues on. With Bayer dithering, thin letters broke up into stray dots (the author didn't like it).
- **Volume:** a single scale from 0 to 200 %. Up to 100 it's the system volume; above that, Audiofilus's boost. Going up, the system volume rises first and then the boost; going down, the reverse. The boost only goes above 100 with the system at 100 % (if the system volume drops from outside, the boost goes back to 100). The service applies it (`volstep`). On Knulli, while the app is open, a wrapper around `knulli-audio` (as Songo did) routes the volume buttons there. The indicator appears on every change (`volume_seq`), whether it comes from the interface (+ and −), the buttons or the CLI.
- **Exiting:** "Go to background" (Y menu and Settings) leaves the music playing (`background`); "Quit" always stops it (`quit`). When the interface closes any other way (closing the window, PortMaster's Select+Start, or the interface dying) it depends on **Settings → Keep playing on close** (`ui.close_to_background`): unset, it's on for the desktop and off on handhelds. The interface keeps an `attach` connection open to the service; with the option on it sends `background` right away and after every attach (attaching resets it; `background off` withdraws it), so when that connection closes the music keeps going. With it off, the service pauses, saves its position and exits. Tested with `kill -9`.
- **Lyrics** (`lyrics.go`, `internal/lyrics`): a view of Now Playing, opened from its More menu or by tapping the visualizer. On top, a small cover, the title, the artist and where the lyrics come from (LRC, TXT, TAG, LRCLIB); in the middle the lyrics; at the bottom the progress bar. Lines use the big font (Jersey 10, 15 px rows, a little more space between lines than between the rows of a wrapped line), and like a carousel they fade out toward the top and bottom of the area in four steps. Synced lyrics keep the current line centered, the lines around it dimmer the farther they are, and the current line fills with the accent color from left to right until the next one starts (karaoke). The current line is drawn letter by letter with a drop shadow and tints pixel by pixel (a letter is half colored while the edge crosses it); the letters at the edge rise in a small wave that runs along it and the line bounces 1 px on each beat. The scroll moves like a spring that overshoots a little, stepped by elapsed time (1/60 s steps), so it looks the same at any frame rate. **Instrumental parts** (`Lyrics.WithBreaks`): an intro of at least 4 s before the first line, an empty LRC line, or a silence of at least 6 s after a line is sung (estimated at 0.4 s per word plus 1 s, between 2 and 7 s) becomes a break shown as three dots; the playing one fills dot by dot until the next line, bounces with the beat and grows just before the voice comes back. Songs LRCLIB marks as instrumental (when no lyrics match) show "INSTRUMENTAL" with the dots. While the lyrics are shown the app never goes to sleep by itself. `-frames` also writes a `letras` sequence (invented lyrics; with `AUDIOFILUS_SHOT_LYRICS=<title>` and `AUDIOFILUS_SHOT_AT=<seconds>` that song from the library with its real lyrics, to look at by hand); ↑/↓ shift them 0.5 s sooner or later (saved per song). Unsynced lyrics scroll with the song's progress, and ↑/↓, a finger or the wheel scroll them by hand for a few seconds. B goes back. They are looked up in the background when the view is open and the song changes, in this order: the version picked by hand; a synced `.lrc` with the song's name (next to it or in a `lyrics`/`Lyrics` folder); synced tags (ID3 `SYLT` in milliseconds, or an LRC inside `USLT`, `©lyr` or the Vorbis `LYRICS`, `SYNCEDLYRICS` and `UNSYNCEDLYRICS` fields); [LRCLIB](https://lrclib.net) (search by title and artist, then without "(feat. ...)", "- Remastered" and similar, then as free text; the best match is the one whose duration is within 4 s, synced preferred); and last the unsynced local ones. Other `.lrc` files in the folder are offered but never picked automatically (they may belong to another song). START → Choose lyrics lists every version (local files, tags and every LRCLIB result, with album and duration) plus "No lyrics"; the choice is saved. `data/lyrics/choices.json` keeps the choice, the offset and when an online search found nothing (retried after a week); LRCLIB lyrics are cached as `data/lyrics/lrclib-<id>.json`, so a song is only looked up once. Settings → Lyrics from the web turns LRCLIB off (`ui.lyrics_offline`). On the radio, the station's title ("Artist - Song") is looked up on LRCLIB, and synced lyrics run from the moment the title changed (stations send it a little late: the offset fixes it, saved per song); titles without that shape (a show name, an ad) and podcasts have none. Requests carry an `audiofilus (https://github.com/...)` User-Agent, as LRCLIB asks.
- **Liked songs, ratings and history** (`marks.go`, `internal/history`): Now Playing's More menu and every song's menu have *Like* and *Rating* (A cycles 1 to 5 and none); a liked song shows a heart in the corner of its cover. They are saved in `data/favorites.json` with the song's title, artist and album (so the list works even for songs from a server that isn't loaded) and, for a server's song, sent to it (`star`/`unstar`/`setRating` in Subsonic, `FavoriteItems` in Jellyfin, which has no ratings). When a server's catalog loads, its starred and rated songs are added (`Marks.Merge`: what is here wins, nothing is removed). The Lists tab of the local library starts with the lists that make themselves: *Liked* (newest first), *Recent* (the last 100 different songs), *Most played* (top 100) and *Stats* (plays, hours since the first listen, the last month, distinct songs and artists, and the top 10 artists, albums and songs; A on a song plays it). They come from `data/history.jsonl`, which the service writes (see below) and the interface reads again only when it changes.
- **Sleep timer:** Now Playing's More menu → *Sleep timer*; each A moves to the next choice (15, 30, 45, 60, 90 minutes, end of the song, off). The service keeps it (`timer <min>|track|off`; `engine.SetStopAfter` stops at the end of the song without crossfading), so it works with the player closed; the cover shows a moon with the minutes left.
- **Listens and scrobbling** (service, `daemon/listens.go`): every second the service checks what is playing and how much of it has really played (no pauses, no seeks). A song counts after half its length or four minutes (if it's at least 30 s): it goes to the history, to Last.fm and ListenBrainz if there's an account (`internal/scrobble`, with the "now playing" five seconds in), and to its Subsonic or Jellyfin server if it's remote (Navidrome forwards to Last.fm/ListenBrainz if set up there). Radios never count; podcast episodes go to the history only. What can't be sent (offline) waits in `data/scrobbles.json` and is retried every two minutes; bad credentials are dropped. Settings → *Scrobbling*: ListenBrainz asks for the user's token (checked with `validate-token`); Last.fm opens its authorization page as a QR and polls `auth.getSession` every 3 s for five minutes. Last.fm needs an API application: the release builds take it from the repository secrets `LASTFM_KEY`/`LASTFM_SECRET` (`-ldflags -X`, see `build.sh`); without them, `scrobble.lastfm_api_key`/`lastfm_secret` in `audiofilus.json`, and otherwise the option says the key is missing. *Count on the server* (on by default) can be turned off.
- **Sound settings** (`sound.go`): *Crossfade* (off to 12 s) in Playback; a *Sound* section with *ReplayGain* (auto, per song, per album, off), *Equalizer* and *Output rate* (not on mobile, where the service can't restart). The equalizer editor shows the ten bands as vertical sliders from -12 to +12 dB: ←/→ pick a band, ↑/↓ move it by 1 dB, X or L1/R1 cycle the presets, Y flattens, A or B close; every change is applied at once (the service saves it), and touching a column picks that band and sets the height touched. Changing the output rate saves it and restarts the service.
- **Radio lists** (`radio/playlist.go`): `.m3u`, `.m3u8` and `.pls` files in the `radio/` folder next to the app (`AUDIOFILUS_HOME`), or the one picked in Settings → *Radio lists* (`ui.radio_dir`), are read when Radio opens and when the *My lists* tab is entered: every `http(s)` address is a station, named by its `#EXTINF` (with its `tvg-logo`) or `TitleN`, or by its host, and tagged with its `group-title` (the category of IPTV-style lists: Pop, News…) or, without one, its list's file name; repeated addresses are dropped. A station's UUID is `url-` plus a hash of its address, so it can be a favorite and goes through the service like the directory's (but isn't reported to radio-browser). START on *Favorites* or *My lists* → *Add by address* asks for an address and a name and adds it to Favorites. When a station's address returns a playlist (`audio/x-scpls`, `mpegurl`, or a `.pls`/`.m3u` address), the stream follows its first address (up to three hops). An HLS playlist (`#EXT-X-`) is played as HLS instead (`radio/hls.go`).
- **Opening files and folders from the file manager** (`cmd/audiofilus/open.go`, the service's `open` command): `audiofilus <files or folders>` (absolute paths or `file://` URIs, as file managers pass them) starts the service if needed and plays them as a temporary queue: folders are expanded like `play` does (CUE sheets, M3U lists, console music songs), and nothing is saved as a playlist ("Save queue as list" in the More menu keeps it). If another `open` arrives within 3 s it is appended instead (Windows starts one process per selected file). If a player window is already open (an `attach` connection), it just sees the new queue and the new process exits; otherwise the player opens on Now Playing. **Linux:** the launcher entry has `Exec=… %F` and a `MimeType` list (every audio format it plays, M3U/PLS, CUE, console music and `inode/directory`), so it shows up in "Open with" for files and folders (`audiofilus desktop install`, which the installer runs). **Windows:** the player registers itself in the user's registry (HKCU, no admin) every time it opens from a new place (`desktop.EnsureRegistered`): a `Audiofilus.Audio` ProgID listed in every extension's `OpenWithProgids`, an `Applications\audiofilus.exe` entry for "Choose another app", and "Play with Audiofilus" on folders; `audiofilus desktop uninstall` removes it. **iOS:** `CFBundleDocumentTypes` (audio, plus imported types for Ogg/Opus, DSD, modules, console music, PLS and CUE) puts it in "Open in" and "Share" from Files and other apps; `AppDelegate` keeps the security-scoped access of files opened in place and passes them to `mobile.OpenFiles`, which waits for the in-process service when the app was launched by the file. Folders can't be shared on iOS: the folder picker in Settings covers them.
- **Desktop:** the service publishes itself as an MPRIS player (`internal/mpris`): media keys and Omarchy's music widget control it with no setup.
- `-shots <folder> -frames <folder>` also saves 8 s of the playback screen, to make a GIF (`ffmpeg -framerate 20 -i f%04d.png ...`).
- **To do:** per-song durations.

To try it: `./build.sh && dist/audiofilus` (the interface starts the service by running its own binary as `audiofilus daemon -detach`; `go run ./cmd/audiofilus` works too). `-shots <folder> -music <m>` saves screenshots of every screen at 1:1, 4:3, 3:2, 16:10, 16:9, 2:1 and 21:9, and in portrait at 3:4 and 9:16, without the service.

The interface will be a separate process, written in Go with the author's Ebitengine fork (the one from Trancazo, with an SDL2 backend for KMS/DRM and no cgo), which talks to the service over the [socket](protocol.md). This document collects what the author asked for before starting it.

## Aesthetics

- **Retro and pixelated, but modern**, like Songo #5: clean, fast and comfortable, not a crude imitation of the 90s.
- **Albums with their covers**, and eye-catching effects.
- **Kage shaders** as in Trancazo: dithering, scanlines, chromatic aberration, transitions. See what can be reused from `trancazo/game/render/`.

## Screens

It must look good and be **readable** at any aspect ratio: 1:1, 4:3, 16:9, 16:10, 3:2, etc. The target handhelds are 640×480 and 720×480 (RG34XX-SP, 3:2) and 720×720; also the PC in a window.

## Games

The minigames share one **Games** entry in the screens dock (`games.go`): a list on the left (icon and name per row, the chosen one marked like in Settings) and the chosen game large on the right, with what it's about and the record from `data/`; ↑/↓ to choose, A to play, B to go back. "Back to games" in each game's pause menu returns there, and the dock highlights Games while any of them is open.

- **Crypt** (`crypt.go`): a rhythm roguelike. Random floors of rooms joined by corridors (44×30 tiles of 12 px), lit around the player, with stairs in the farthest room. A step (or an attack, stepping into an enemy) only counts within `min(0.17 s, 35 % of the period)` of a beat; off the beat it's a MISS. The game keeps its own beat grid, which follows the one from `meter.go` smoothly (tempo and phase a little each frame; the tempo at once only when it really changes, like a new song), and it remembers beats by their time, not their number: the meter's numbering restarts when it re-anchors. Enemies act when each beat's window closes, after the player: slimes bounce every two beats, bats flutter at random every beat, skeletons raise their arms one beat and chase the next (armored ones, from depth 3, take two hits). On-beat steps in a row raise the multiplier (×2 every 8, FEVER ×4 at 24); a MISS or a hit taken resets it, standing still doesn't. Kills drop coins; hearts heal up to five. The floor flashes in a checker on each beat with the cover's colors (two accents in FEVER), and behind the dungeon a faint sigil listens to the music: a ring and a seven-pointed star that turn and throb with the bass, twelve runes lit by the spectrum bands, and rising embers. The rhythm bar shows the next beats closing in on a heart, the hit window around it, "GOOD" or "MISS" after each step; the heart and the floor beat on the game's own clock, at the same moment the marks arrive. With the music paused the game freezes; without a tempo yet it uses a 110 BPM metronome. Record in `data/crypt.json`. `cryptbot.go` plays it for the screenshots and the website animation.
- **Tracker** (`trackerui.go` for state and input, `trackerdraw.go` for drawing; engine in `internal/tracker`, design in [tracker.md](tracker.md)): while it plays, its output goes through the same analyser as the music (`engine.Analyzer`, tapped by `meterTap`), so the visualizers, the beat-following games and the background react to it (`App.meterPts`, `App.hearing`). Eight views in a row like LSDJ's map: project (name, Game Boy or Free mode, tempo, starting groove), song (rows × channels of chain numbers, with a live oscilloscope per channel), chain, phrase (note, instrument, volume and two commands per step; X switches to a PICO-8 style piano roll), instrument, table, groove and wave. L1/R1 move between views and R1 opens what is under the cursor. Every grid edits the same way through `tkField`/`tkEdit`: A held plus ↑↓ changes by one and ←→ by a lot; A on an empty cell puts the last value used and a quick second A puts a new unused one; B+A empties; B+←→ picks another chain, phrase, instrument...; B+↑↓ jumps 16 song rows. Y plays or stops what the view shows (the song from the cursor row, the chain or the phrase looping on the current channel) and pauses the music; B+Y mutes the channel. Notes sound when written (`Player.Preview`), playing rows and notes light up (`Player.Position`, `Player.Now`, `Player.Hits`). Start: play the song, undo (a JSON snapshot per A press, up to 64), copy, paste, clone into the first free number (repointing the cell you came from), clear, PICO-8 wave presets, songs, **export as WAV, XM or MOD to the music folder** (`audiofilus tracker/`, then the library rescans), **Import…** (`trackerfiles.go`: walks `data/tracker/import` and the music folders, up to 300 `.mod`/`.xm`/`.lsdsng`/`.sav`, in the background; each imported song is saved as a new one, a `.sav` gives several), **Search online…** (MOD/XM from The Mod Archive and Modland, downloaded and imported), delete. **MIDI** (`trackermidi.go`, `internal/midi`): while the tracker is open, `midi.Watch` re-scans the devices every 2 s and the first one (or the one picked in PROJ → MIDI) is opened; incoming notes preview the last instrument on the current channel and, in the phrase view, are written at the cursor (which moves down a step; a velocity under full sets the step volume, 0–F). PROJ → Sync: internal, follow the incoming clock (`Player.SetExternalClock`, pulses go straight to `ExternalTick` from the reader goroutine) or send ours while playing (`Player.ClockOut`). Leaving the tracker (not playing) stops watching and closes the device. The synth is an `io.Reader` streamed through the interface's own audio output (`playStream` in `sounds.go`, 50 ms buffer). Songs are saved in `data/tracker/` (one `.json` each, `current` names the open one) a second after each change; old-format songs are converted on load. Start → **Songs** lists new Game Boy and Free songs, the saved ones and four examples (`internal/tracker/examples.go`: Pocket Pop, Crypt Crawl, Night Drive, Amen Chip); opening an example saves nothing until it's edited (then as `example-*.json`).
- **Name that song** (`quiz.go`): ten rounds; each loads a random local song (audiobooks left out) through the service, seeks to 0:35 and offers it among four (other titles), in a 2×2 grid. Ten seconds per round; points for the time left, a streak multiplier (×2 from the fourth in a row). Record in `data/quiz.json`. Pauses the music at the end.
- **Snake** (`snake.go`): one step per subdivision of the beat (quarter, eighth or sixteenth, the closest to 1/8 s), from the smooth grid; coins grow it, a heart may appear on downbeats for three beats. Record in `data/snake.json`.
- **Ships** (`shooter.go`): the ship fires on every eighth note, three shots in a fan when the beat has bass; enemies arrive with the beats (more as the score grows), shooters fire every two beats. Three lives. Record in `data/ships.json`. `gamebots.go` plays Snake and Ships for the screenshots and website.
- **Runner** (`runner.go`): an auto-runner modeled on Chromium's offline dinosaur. One hit ends the run (A restarts). A or ↑ jumps; holding it gives a full jump (30 half-resolution pixels, exactly one beat long: gravity is derived from the tempo), releasing early cuts it short after a minimum hop, and ↓ in the air drops fast (×3 gravity). The world scrolls a fixed distance per beat, rising from 36 to 56 px with the distance run; the speed eases toward that target, so neither a tempo re-estimate nor a score bonus makes it jump. Each obstacle is planned for a beat (on the current grid) but then lives at a fixed spot in the world (`ruThing.d`), so the scenery only ever scrolls (`TestRunnerScrollsSmoothly` changes the tempo and adds a bonus mid-run); the beat marks on the ground are world things too. They come from the drum **pattern** like Rhythm (`beat.drums.at`): a kick (or cymbal) on a beat brings a speaker (one, two side by side, or a tall amp), a snare a flying vinyl at one of three heights (low: jump it; middle: duck under it, or jump; high: harmless unless you jump into it). At least a beat and a half separates two obstacles (a full jump and its landing). Notes only go where they can be caught: at the apex of the full jump above a speaker, or at ground level on off-beat hi-hats far from any obstacle (`TestRunnerReachable` checks every case against the real hitboxes, `ruHeroBox` and `box`). Jumping on the eighth before an obstacle (the "and") puts the apex on it: PERFECT, raising a multiplier up to ×8. The hero is a 12×12 sprite with headphones and a two-frame run (one step per eighth), a long low duck pose and a crossed-eyes crash frame. Score in five digits with the record ("HI"), blinking at every hundred. Behind, the Plasma visualizer (`plasmaInto`) mapped to the cover's palette, dimmed so the foreground reads, and a horizon line that vibrates with the spectrum; the ground has scrolling pebbles and a mark per beat. Drawn at half resolution and scaled ×2. Record in `data/runner.json`; `botRunner` plays it for the screenshots and website.
- **Duel** (`duel.go`): turn-based battles. Your turn: a cursor crosses the attack bar in two beats, timed by the beat clock: it leaves on a beat, so its center lands exactly on the next one (the bar marks each beat and eighth); A hits harder near the center (up to 8), ×1.5 on the beat. Their turn, eight beats: a heart dodges, inside a 160×80 box, the notes each foe throws on every beat. Notes move in surges, fast on the beat and slowing until the next one, and the song's drums (`beat.drums`) add more: each snare sends notes aimed at the heart from the top corners, a cymbal a fan from the foe, and the kick shakes the box; the box border pulses on every beat. The patterns: the scratched vinyl, rotating rings; the tangled tape, sweeping lines with a gap; the metronome, columns falling on alternating sides (its pendulum swings with the grid). With bass, one more note aimed at the heart. Beaten foes come back tougher. The foes are 1-bit sprites (one color, details as holes). Record in `data/duel.json`; `botDuel` plays it for the screenshots and website.
- **Blocks** (`tetris.go`) and **Rhythm** (`rhythm.go`): both use the same smooth beat grid (`steadyBeat` in `meter.go`, with `followGrid`, which Crypt also uses), so Blocks' beat bar doesn't jump when the meter corrects itself, and Rhythm places each new note on the current grid instead of adding half beats one after another (a correction used to leave every later note out of step). Rhythm's lanes follow the detected drums (`drumLane`): kick, snare, and the hi-hats alternating eighth by eighth between the two right lanes, with crashes on the last one (crashes alone left it almost empty). On a portrait screen (phones) the side panel moves to a strip on top (what plays, score, combo, accuracy and best) and the lanes are centered below it, as wide as they fit (`drawRhythmHead`).

Pitch follows the speed like a real turntable, near standstill it goes silent and slow movement sounds muffled (a one-pole filter that opens with speed); afterwards playback resumes exactly where it was heard, playing first the generated audio the flush threw away. ## Splash, Settings and screen off

- **Closing the lid enters sleep** immediately, with no fade, on any screen (`lidClosed`: Insert on Anbernics with a lid; not on the PC). Opening it doesn't wake: Start does. Not when the service reports an external display (`status.docked`: HDMI connected), so you can play docked with the lid shut.
- **The sleep timer only counts on the playback screen** (not while browsing or in Settings). The console's volume buttons, which don't go through the interface, also reset the timer (via `VolumeSeq`).
- **Boost with limiter**: above 100 % the system stays at 100 and the service amplifies its signal up to ×2 with a peak limiter (`internal/engine/limiter.go`: instant attack, 80 ms release, -0.2 dB ceiling). This way songs mastered very loud get louder without hard clipping. At 100 % or below the signal passes through untouched.
- **Volume indicator**: slides in from the top only if it wasn't visible; if it already is, the number and the bar change. The service notifies the interface instantly over the `attach` connection (`control.Notify`) when the volume or sleep changes, and applies the system volume in the background (knulli-audio takes ~100 ms per call).
- **Splash** (`internal/ui/splash.go`): "Audiofilus" drops letter by letter with a bounce, waves, an LED equalizer pulses and the signature appears (GitHub logo + juanvillacortac). It lasts 2.8 s, any button skips it and the library loads behind it. With `-frames` it's saved to `frames/splash/`.
- **Settings** is a mosaic of tiles grouped by section (Library, Playback, Screen, System, Exit). Toggles: A flips them. Choices: all options show as chips if they fit (otherwise, the value and one dot per option); A opens the choice, left/right pick immediately, A or B close it. Volume: the same, in steps of 10. Actions (folder, rescan, quit): A. The Library section also has *Radio lists* and *MilkDrop presets*: A opens the same folder browser as the music folder (`folders.go`, `pickFor` says what the pick is for) and the folder is saved as `ui.radio_dir` / `ui.milkdrop_dir`; empty means `radio/` and `milkdrop/` next to the app. Both are hidden on iPhone. The arrows go to the nearest tile in that direction, so the mosaic works with 2 or 3 columns depending on width.
- **Screen off**: entering sleep, the screen dissolves to black with an 8×8 Bayer pattern (0.5 s; touching anything cancels it) and on return it undissolves in 0.25 s (`dissolveSrc` in `shaders.go`; with `-frames` in `frames/sleep/`). The **sleep mode** (`ui.sleep_mode` in `audiofilus.json`) is `backlight` (the service turns off the backlight with `knulli-brightness dispoff`; on the PC it does nothing) or `oled` (it only paints black: `sleep on oled`). In both, idlewatcher stops and gestures work.
- **Headphones in the top bar**: every 2 s, and only when asked for the state, the service checks the audio output in the background (the state never waits for it; `refreshOutput`): PulseAudio's default output (`pactl info` and `pactl list sinks`, in `platform/output.go`). On handhelds with the H700 jack pin it skips `pactl list sinks` (with HDMI it froze the picture): the pin says wired or speaker and the default sink's name says bluetooth. A bluez output (or `device.bus = "bluetooth"`) is bluetooth. An active port or an output that mentions headphones (headphone, headset) is wired, unless the port says "not available". The `output` field of the state is `speaker`, `wired`, `bluetooth` or `""`. With the speaker, nothing is drawn. On the RG34XX-SP (H700) PulseAudio doesn't see the jack; there it reads the kernel's "Headphone detection" pin in `/sys/kernel/debug/gpio` (high = plugged in), which Knulli mounts at boot. Verified by plugging and unplugging.
- **Battery in the top bar**: every 5 s the interface reads the system battery in `/sys/class/power_supply` (type `Battery`, not a controller's), with its level and status (`status`). The level comes from the charge counter (`charge_now/charge_full` or `energy_now/energy_full`) when there is one, since some gauges give a wrong `capacity` (the RK817 on dArkOS); otherwise `capacity`, and with 0 % an estimate from the voltage. With `Charging` or `Full` a bolt appears next to the battery and the level steps up to full, in a loop (whenever the screen redraws).
- **Album grid**: covers are between 48 and 64 px and are spread to fill the width (`gridLayout`): 4 columns at 1:1, 4:3 and 3:2, and 5 at 16:9.
- **Testing formats with gamescope**: inside gamescope (`GAMESCOPE_WAYLAND_DISPLAY`) the interface starts fullscreen and takes its format, e.g. `gamescope -W 720 -H 480 -- dist/audiofilus` for 3:2. Before, it stayed in a 4:3 window with black bars.
- **Sleep without rendering**: after painting black, the interface switches to `FPSModeVsyncOffMinimum`. Ebitengine stops producing frames: it doesn't call Update or Draw or present anything, and the black stays on screen. It only wakes on an input event or when the client sees that the service left sleep (`Client.OnWake` → `ebiten.ScheduleFrame`). OnWake also sets a flag (`App.woke`) that exits sleep: with frames stopped, the interface never gets to see the service *in* sleep and can't infer from the state change that it woke up (that's how the screen used to stay on and black). On handhelds the keyboard doesn't wake it: on the RG34XX-SP the lid sends Insert/Delete through the same device as the gamepad, and closing it was waking it. In sleep the state is polled every second instead of every 250 ms, and the service doesn't check the audio output. On the PC the interface went from several % CPU to ~0.2 %. On the console it still needs measuring: the fork's SDL2 backend keeps polling events at 60 Hz in that mode.
- **Progress bar with transition**: when seeking forward or back, the bar and the time travel the jump in ~0.25 s with easing (`tweenPosition`). The interface assumes the jump happened (`seekBy`) while the service responds. On song change, the bar jumps with no transition.
- **Real visualizer**: the service computes the spectrum of what's playing (1024-sample FFT with a Hann window, 16 logarithmic bands from 40 Hz to 16 kHz, `engine/meter.go`), the level and the bass, every ~21 ms, along with the time it will be heard (now plus two audio buffers: the player's and oto's context's). Only with the app open and awake. The interface receives it with the state and draws it on time (`ui/meter.go`); each bar highlights what changes relative to the last second (modern mixes sit near the top almost all the time), rises instantly, falls slowly and leaves a peak marker.
- **The background reacts to the music** (`bgSrc`, `App.updatePump`): the first light blob grows and brightens with the bass, the second with the treble (they rise fast and fall slowly), and each beat sends out a ripple from the first. On every screen: strongly on Now Playing and Rhythm, and at ~55 % on the rest so it doesn't get in the way of reading. It's still quantized and dithered as always. The beat (`updateBeat`) is tracked once per frame for the whole app (`App.beatHit`).
- **Buttons on every screen** (since October 18, 2026): **Start opens the screen's "more" menu** (the one for what's playing, for the selection in the library or the results, the pause menu in the games, the visualizer list), **Y pauses or resumes** (it used to be the other way round), L1/R1 change song and L2/R2 seek back or forward.
- **Screen bar** (`dock.go`): Select opens a row of icons (Now Playing, Library, Search, Queue, Visualizer, Downloads —only with yt-dlp—, Blocks, Rhythm, Settings) that slides up from the bottom, with the selected one's name above it and a dot under the current one. When it opens it's already on the **previous screen**, so pressing Select twice switches between the last two, like Alt+Tab; ←/→ choose, Select or A go, and B or Start close. **B returns** to the previous screen in Queue, Settings, Visualizer and Downloads (on Now Playing, B still goes to the library). It replaces cycling through the screens in a row with Select, double Select, and holding Select (they weren't practical with seven screens).
- **Controls guide** (`guide.go`): while **FN** is held (F1 on the keyboard) it shows, in two columns, what each button does on that screen and on all screens, and how to use sleep (its gestures) and background mode (the FN ×3 shortcut); releasing it closes it.
- **Double Start: sleep.** The first Start opens the "more" menu; if the second comes within 0.35 s, the menu closes and the screen turns off (`doubleStart`, in `menu.go`).
- **Logo** (`logo.go`): 2000s media player look, without copying Winamp's lightning bolt or flame: a shiny CD (chrome rim, grooves, spindle and a rotating glint) and "Audiofilus" in chrome letters, pixel-bold and pixel-italic, with outline, bevel, a sweeping shine, star sparkles and a curved underline. The CD is drawn pixel by pixel (`WritePixels`) and the letters come from a mask: bevel and outline are the shifted mask, and the chrome is the mask row by row with each row's color. It's used by the splash and the PortMaster cover (which puts the logo on top and the vinyl and tagline below). While FN is held no other button is handled, because Knulli uses FN+button for its shortcuts. In the button pills (the bottom help bar and the guide), arrows are drawn as chevrons (`hint_l`, `hint_r`, `hint_u`, `hint_d`), not with the font's "<" and ">".
- **Transition** (`transition.go`): on screen change, the new one slides in from one side and pushes the old one out (0.16 s, with easing, in whole pixels, with an accent stripe on its edge): from the right if it comes later in the screen bar, from the left if it comes earlier. (There was a dithered curtain; it was slow and nobody liked it.)
- **Rhythm** (`rhythm.go`): a lane game. Notes fall down four lanes, one per drum instrument (kick, snare, hi-hat, cymbals, on Y B X A), and reach the line on the tempo grid. Since they have to appear 1.3 s before they sound and the meter only sees half a second ahead, they come from the drum **pattern**: which eighth note of the bar each instrument hit in the last two bars (`beat.drums`). Music repeats its patterns, so the notes follow the song and change with it (before there's a pattern, one note per beat). Each instrument is recognized by how much its bands rise, with its own threshold; a treble hit much louder than the usual hi-hat is a cymbal. Minimalist look: thin lines, flat notes and each lane's button on its receptor (the arrow if the keyboard is in use). One note at a time (priority kick, snare, cymbal, hi-hat), two-handed with neighboring buttons: ← ↓ on the D-pad and B A (↑ → Y X as an alternative; D F J K on the keyboard) —with the whole diamond you had to jump between opposites—; windows of ±50/100/150 ms (PERFECT, GREAT, GOOD); a combo that multiplies, FEVER at 50, accuracy, max combo and high score in `data/rhythm.json`. Start pauses (resume, pause the music, new game, quit), Select changes screen, L1/R1 change song. Hits are felt: the receptor flashes white and then the lane's color, a beam shoots up the lane, a square ring grows and, on PERFECT, sparks fly; the line lights up and the judgment appears big, with a bounce and its color, with the combo below. Pressing with no note only highlights the receptor's edge; a missed note dims it and makes it shake. With no tempo yet, "Listening for the beat...".
- **Visualizer** (`visual.go`): one screen with nine visualizers (the last one is MilkDrop, see [milkdrop.md](milkdrop.md)) (←/→ switch, A camera or variant, Start the list to pick from, Y pause, B back). Each one draws in intensity and `paletteSrc` maps it onto the palette ramp (background → accent → text) with dithering: a single color. Drift and Trench at full resolution; the rest at half (chunky pixels). Each one has its own 7×7 icon (`visIcons`, drawn in `icons.go`) in the Start list. The choice is remembered: `setVisualizer` saves its name (`ui.visualizer`, e.g. `"lake"`) and `visualizerIndex` restores it at startup (an unknown name falls back to the first; the default is `"milk"`, MilkDrop). `stepVisual` advances the scenes (Drift, Trench, Lake) and MilkDrop's automatic preset change one 1/60 s step; it runs on this screen and, when the visualizer is the player's background, on Now Playing. **Visualizer background** (Now Playing → Start, `ui.visual_bg`, on by default and saved even when off, so turning it off sticks): `drawVisualBG` renders the chosen visualizer into its own canvas without its name or hints (`vis.bg`) and blends it over the theme background at `visualBGAlpha` (0.32), before the player draws its cover, texts and controls; MilkDrop is forced to album colors there (`milkPalette`), and it is skipped while lyrics are open (`visualBGOn`).
  - **Drift** (`drift.go`): an original low-poly coupe (cabin, pop-up headlights, full-width taillight, rims, wheel arches; no brands) drifts along a road made of music: a new curve every bar, tighter the louder it plays; speed follows the tempo, speeding up and slowing down gradually; streetlights that flash; the city in the background is the spectrum and the sun pulses with the bass. It dances: bounces with the kick, sways with the snare, jumps with the cymbals; smoke when drifting. Three cameras.
  - **Bars** (Winamp), **Tunnel** (feedback in the style of MilkDrop/WMP's Battery), **Plasma** (WMP's Ambience), **Waves** (oscilloscope with phosphor). The waves are synthesized from the spectrum (the service doesn't send the waveform).
  - **Trench** (`trench.go`): an original fighter (delta wing, two engines) flies through the trench of a station whose modules come from the spectrum; it zigzags, banks with the snare, barrel-rolls with the cymbals, engines that pulse with the bass, shots on each beat from the cannons under the wings, turrets that shoot at it.
  - **Ridges** (`ridges.go`): the stacked lines of the *Unknown Pleasures* cover. Every 1/12 s a new line holds the spectrum of that moment plus its own noise; lines enter at the bottom and rise, and each one blacks out what's below its curve, so it hides the ones behind (all of it in one `rectBatch`: a fill and a line per column). Lines that entered on a beat glow brighter. Variant 0: bass in the center, in peaks, like the pulsar; variant 1: bass to treble, left to right.
  - **MilkDrop** (`milkdrop.go`, `internal/milkdrop`): MilkDrop 1 presets (`.milk`), in color, with its own controls (L1/R1 preset, A lock) and a Colors button that switches between the preset's colors and the album's. See [milkdrop.md](milkdrop.md).
  - Own 3D engine (`r3d` in `drift.go`): projection, flat shading with a height gradient on the car and the ship, near-plane clipping, layered painter's algorithm, a single `DrawTriangles`.
- **The vinyl follows seeks**: when seeking forward or back it spins in that direction as much as it would spin playing those seconds.
- **Blocks** (`tetris.go`): one more screen in the Select rotation. Switching screens keeps the game. Mechanics of modern games, like Apotris: SRS rotation with its wall kicks, DAS/ARR (10 and 2 frames), soft drop of one row every 2 frames, 0.5 s lock delay with 15 move resets (reaching lower restores them), hold (X, once per piece), three next pieces visible, two hidden rows at the top, gravity following the standard curve, T-spins (three-corner rule) and back-to-back (×1.5). Music beats no longer drop the piece (it broke the flow); lines clear on the beat if it comes within 0.25 s, otherwise right away.
- **The beat** (`meter.go`, `beat`): beats from spectral flux (how much the bass and mid bands rise relative to the previous point, with an adaptive threshold), tempo from the median of the intervals folded into 70–180 BPM (it changes only if the new one is confirmed) and a beat grid that keeps going even if the music drops; detected beats correct its phase. All on real time (not frame time). It resets on song change. Flat pieces with clipped corners, in the theme's colors. It reacts to the music: on each bass beat the piece drops a row, bounces a pixel, the well hops slightly and its border flashes; behind the pieces faint columns rise with the spectrum, and stacked pieces light up by column; the tempo is computed (BPM, median of the intervals between beats) and dropping a piece within ±0.1 s of a beat is worth double ("ON BEAT!"); clearing lines makes the background pump. It's a rhythm game: a **lane** in the panel shows the upcoming beats (from the tempo) approaching a line; dropping on time raises the **combo** (×2, ×3…, multiplies the points) and off-beat breaks it; at ×4 you enter **FEVER** (double points, a border alternating the accents, a more intense background). Full lines blink and **explode on the next beat**, with a horizontal ripple in the background; when a piece is dropped, a **laser** crosses the well where it landed. The background is a rhythm shader (`rhythmSrc`): square rings coming out of the center on each beat, flash, scanlines and the usual dithering. In the mockup (`-shots`, `-frames`) the meter is made up, at 120 BPM. On each beat a **wave of light** climbs the stack from the bottom of the well, like Tetris Effect: it lightens the blocks it passes, grows them up to 2 px on each side and lights their centers; it always takes 0.3 s to climb, whatever the stack's height, and is stronger the louder the music (with the second accent in FEVER). When a piece locks, a **beam** shoots up from each of its columns, like Apotris: it rises from the piece's highest cell, fades upward and narrows to the column's center as it dies out (0.35 s, in steps); a hard drop's beam reaches the top of the well, a piece that fell on its own gets six rows. **Sounds**, like Tetris Effect (`sfx/blocks.go`): soft bells on the pentatonic scale of the key of what's playing (major: 1 2 3 5 6, minor: 1 ♭3 4 5 ♭7; no semitones to clash with the song; C major until the key is known). The key comes from the service (`engine/key.go`, in the status as `key`, or the tracker's analyzer): every 8192 frames (~0.17 s) a long FFT (~6 Hz bins) adds up how much each note sounds between 100 and 2500 Hz (a chromagram, with ~30 s of memory, reset on each song), compared with the Krumhansl–Kessler profiles of the 24 keys; it needs ~2.5 s of music, and runs only with the meter (the interface open and awake). When it hesitates it's usually between a key and its relative (F# and D#m), which share the same five pentatonic notes. The sound names carry the key (`bk_move_3@F#m`). moving plays the column's note (higher to the right; one note in three while auto-repeating), rotating two notes of the rotation state, hold two falling notes, a lock a low thud, a hard drop a deeper boom with a chime (two chimes on the beat), and clearing lines an arpeggio of n+2 notes (with an open chord on a TETRA), which plays when the lines explode, on the beat. A **narrator** with a deep, robotic voice calls the plays: DOUBLE, TRIPLE, TETRA, T-SPIN (SINGLE/DOUBLE/TRIPLE), BACK TO BACK, LEVEL UP, FEVER and GAME OVER, and the big on-screen call shows the same words. The voice is synthesized with no audio files (`sfx/voice.go`): a small Klatt-style formant synthesizer, with Rosenberg glottal pulses at about 85 Hz falling to 65 (rising a little on stressed syllables) through three cascaded formant resonators lowered 10 % (a bigger voice), filtered noise for S, F and the bursts of P/T/K, silent closures with formant loci for the stops, and a light retro color (samples held at 24 kHz, 8 bits), plus a stadium echo: four repeats 0.19 s apart, each 45 % quieter and darker (one-pole low-pass at 2.5 kHz), alternating left and right. Game sounds and the narrator use the music sounds volume (Settings → Sounds). Controls: arrows, ↓ fast, ↑ drop, A rotates right and B left, X holds, Start opens the pause menu (resume, new game, quit), Select opens the screen bar; the music, without leaving: Y pause, L1/R1 song, L2/R2 seek. With a keyboard (no gamepad or touch in use; `tetKeys`) it plays like modern games instead: Z/X (also ↑ and Ctrl) rotate, Space hard-drops, C or Shift hold, Esc or M pause, P pauses the music; the bottom help bar and the F1 guide name those keys. The high score goes in `data/blocks.json`. It isn't named after the original game, which is a trademark.
- **Juice on the playback screen** (`juice.go`): on song change the cover slides in from the side (next: right; previous: left) with a bounce and a stepped flash, and the vinyl gets a burst of speed (scratch); pausing or resuming makes the button "pop" with a growing ring, and the previous or next button does the same on song change; seeking forward or back nudges the bar in that direction. Pixel confetti was tried and removed (nobody liked it). With `-frames` there's a sample of all three.
- **Menu (Y)** (`menu.go`): a panel that slides up from the bottom with a short bounce. On top, the cover, title and artist of the selection; below, a mosaic of icon buttons (3 columns on narrow screens, 4 on wide ones) navigated with the arrows, like Settings. Toggle buttons (Shuffle, Repeat) light up when active, show their value and don't close the menu; Quit has a different color. The bottom help bar switches to the menu's.
- **Changing song without triggers**: on the playback screen, a tap of left/right goes to the previous/next song and holding it seeks back/forward without steps, accelerating (`tapOrHold`, `scrubSpeed`): after 0.3 s held the position starts moving at 2 s of song per second (one second at a time, to fine-tune) and speeds up by 20 s/s every second held, up to 50 s/s. The progress bar follows it smoothly; the service gets the position every 0.2 s and on release (`sendScrub`, absolute `seek`: each one flushes the audio buffer), and only the start of the hold makes a sound. L1/R1 still change song. That way everything works on consoles without triggers. L1/R1 and L2/R2 also work with the Start menu or the Select bar open (`overlayMusicKeys`): neither uses them. In the service each station is a `decode.Source`: a goroutine reads from the network into a buffer (also from old Shoutcast servers that answer "ICY 200 OK"), extracts the title of what's playing from the ICY metadata and reconnects if it drops. **HLS** stations (`radio/hls.go`) go into the same buffer: from a master playlist it takes the separate audio rendition or the best variant up to 400 kbps, reloads the live media playlist every target duration (starting from its last three segments), downloads the new segments in order (also byte ranges of a single file, `#EXT-X-BYTERANGE`), decrypts AES-128 ones, and pulls the audio out: AAC (ADTS) or MP3 from MPEG-TS (looking for the sync byte even if something comes first) or packed-audio (ID3 + ADTS/MP3) segments, and AAC from fMP4 segments (`#EXT-X-MAP`: the init segment gives the track and its AudioSpecificConfig, `decode.ParseFMP4Init`/`FMP4Samples`, and each frame gets an ADTS header, with the core profile and rate for HE-AAC). A segment that fails once is retried with the next playlist without losing its place; more than five in a row, it reconnects from the start. The title comes from `#EXTINF` when the station puts one there. AAC in LATM inside TS and other codecs in fMP4 (Opus, FLAC, AC-3) give a clear "unsupported" error instead of silence; the audio thread decodes from that buffer with the "feed" mode of libmpg123 or libfdk-aac (`decode/stream.go`) without ever waiting: if data is missing, silence until there's ~1.5 s of margin (and the bottom bar says "connecting to the station"). ## The Trancazo recipe (starting point)

Trancazo already solves this with the same fork; it's worth copying its approach (see `trancazo/docs/dibujo.md` and `trancazo/game/render/`):

- **Scaling with hard pixels** (`App.DrawFinalScreen`, `ebiten.FilterPixelated`): fullscreen or in a window of any size the scale isn't an integer, and the default filter smoothed everything; with this one, pixels look hard and the same size at any scale, without the black bars that limiting to integer scales would leave.
- **Integer scale on the desktop** (`Framed`, `framed.go`, only when the service reports the `desktop` platform): a non-integer scale still leaves soft pixel edges, so a window whose short side is at least 480 physical pixels gets the largest integer scale (×2, ×3…, counting the HiDPI factor) and the canvas becomes the whole window at that scale (a maximized 1366×768 window is 671×359 at ×2), with no bars. The canvas is rounded down and `Framed.DrawFinalScreen` draws it at exactly that scale, centered, so a size that isn't a multiple (1366 at ×3 is 455 plus one pixel) leaves a pixel of background at the edge instead of stretching to ×3.002. Smaller windows get the exact (non-integer, pixelated) scale that makes the short side 240, since ×1 would be tiny, and the canvas is still the whole window, without `ViewSize`'s 21:9 cap, so a very wide window has no side bars; handhelds are untouched (their screens are already integer). Screens lay out to any size; Now Playing grows the cover (and the vinyl) with the extra height (`128 + 3/4` of what exceeds 240), and goes side by side only when the canvas is at least 1.2 times wider than tall, so a near-square window gets the vertical layout with the big centered cover. On a canvas wider than 21:9 the cover, vinyl and longest text line are centered as a group (`off`, text at most a 21:9 block wide), while the spectrum and the progress bar still span the whole width.
- **The short side is always 240 px, and the orientation decides the rest** (`App.Layout` with `ViewSize`), always pixel art scaled without smoothing. In portrait (a phone, a narrow window) the canvas is 240 wide and as tall as the proportion asks, up to 560 (9:16 → 240×427, 3:4 → 240×320): Now Playing grows the cover to fill the width (with the vinyl peeking out) and adds the album under the artist; lists, Settings and menus simply show more; Blocks, Snake and Duel center in the height; the controls guide goes to one column when it fits; the splash logo drops to ×1 where ×2 doesn't fit. In landscape, **a fixed logical height of 240 px and the width from the window** (`ViewW`, like `world.AspectFor` in Trancazo): any width between 240 (1:1) and 560 (21:9), not just fixed formats; anything wider than 21:9 stays at that with bars. For example, 1:1 → 240, 4:3 → 320, 3:2 → 360, 16:10 → 384, 16:9 → 427, 2:1 → 480, 21:9 → 560. 640×480 is 320×240 ×2, 720×480 is 360×240 ×2 and 720×720 is 240×240 ×3: integer scaling on all three handhelds.
- **The essentials inside the central 240×240 square**, which is visible in every format; the extra space on the sides is for decoration or wider lists.
- **Pixel mouse cursor on the desktop** (`mousecursor.go`, drawn by `Framed.DrawFinalScreen`): the system cursor is hidden over the window and the interface draws an 8×12 arrow in the style of old emulators (ZSNES), with the same pixel size as the canvas, a white highlight, the album (or theme) color for its body and a one-pixel shadow. It shows when the mouse moves or clicks, and hides on a key or controller button, when the window loses focus, and when the mouse leaves the window (`ebiten.IsCursorInWindow`, added to our Ebitengine fork). Besides matching the rest, it doesn't depend on the desktop's cursor: through XWayland a window without a cursor of its own inherits the root one, which Omarchy 4.5 leaves blank after its startup.
- **Final pass** (`render.DrawFinal`, `finalShaderSrc`): "sharp bilinear" (nearest neighbor at integer scale, blending only at the edges otherwise), and an option for integer scaling with black borders.
- **Pixel fonts at their native size**, never scaled with `GeoM`: Jersey 10 for the interface, Tiny5 at 8 px for long text; `crispFace` leaves each pixel fully on or off. They cover accents, ñ, ¡ and ¿.
- **Reusable shaders:** `limitSrc` (4×4 Bayer dithering and color modes), `tv()` (scanlines and vignette), `avShaderSrc` (chroma aberration), `menuBgShaderSrc` and `sunburstShaderSrc` (animated backgrounds), `pageTurnShaderSrc` (transition), `gaugeShaderSrc` (progress and volume bars), `pixelZoomShaderSrc` (mosaic). Everything quantized with Bayer so gradients come out dithered rather than banded.
- **Mali rules:** avoid per-pixel `sin`/`cos`/`atan2`, reuse `Unmanaged` canvases (`canvas.go`) and warm up shaders and canvases on the loading screen (`Warmup`). Every new shader gets a compile test.

For power saving: outside pocket mode, animated effects cost CPU and GPU every frame; at rest (nothing changing on screen) the interface should stop redrawing.

## Memory: it must work with 512 MB

The RG34XX-SP has 2 GB, but Audiofilus must work on 512 MB handhelds, alongside the system, the frontend and the audio service.

- **Original covers aren't kept.** A decoded 1000×1000 cover is 4 MB; keeping them pushed the mockup to 926 MB after scrolling through 408 albums. Now each cover is decoded, downscaled to the size it's drawn at, and the original is released (`Album.decodeCover`). Only its two colors remain.
- **Downscaled covers have a limit** (`maxCovers`, 192, LRU): the ones unseen the longest are freed from the GPU.
- **`debug.SetMemoryLimit(96 MB)`** in the interface, so the garbage collector returns memory sooner.
- **Benchmarking a visualizer:** `dist/audiofilus -music <folder> -bench lake [-benchprof f.prof]` (or `drift`, `trench`, `bars`...) draws 600 frames without the service, with the mockup's beat (a third with each camera, two 1/60 steps per draw, as at 30 fps), and reports how long the CPU takes per frame, how much memory it allocates and how many triangles it produces; `-benchprof` saves the CPU and memory profiles (`f.prof.mem`). On the PC (October 18, 2026): Lake went from 2.56 ms and 136 KB per frame to 1.2 ms and 59 KB (the water height is computed once per mesh point instead of four times, per-ripple work once per frame, and ripples are cut off where they're no longer visible; the engine sorts indices instead of triangles, and `r3d.box` doesn't allocate); Drift from 0.85 to 0.57 ms; Trench ~1 ms. The frames come out pixel-identical.
- **Measuring:** `go run ./cmd/audiofilus -music <folder> -scrolltest` scrolls through all albums and shows memory. On the PC (October 17, 2026, 408 albums): 114 MB at startup, 153 MB peak; the Go heap, 22 MB peak. The rest is the PC's OpenGL driver (Mesa); **it still needs measuring on the console** (Mali with SDL2/KMS).

## Smoothness

- Covers are prepared by two workers (`covers.go`); the interface thread only uploads the ready ones to the GPU, and they appear with a fade. Doing it while drawing caused stutters when switching screens and scrolling. Now Playing also requests, at its size, the covers of the next three songs in the queue from other albums (`prefetchCovers`), so the next one shows at once instead of after the placeholder (it took up to a second).
- The row above and the row below in the grid are requested ahead of time.
- Shaders are warmed up on the first frame (`warmUp`), as in Trancazo.
- Truncations of long titles are cached (`fit`).

## Features: at least those of Songo #5

Taken from Songo's code (see the analysis in the project memory):

- **Library:** all songs, albums, artists, playlists and folders; import at startup; embedded covers and covers fetched online (MusicBrainz, Fanart.tv).
- **Playlists:** `.m3u`, create and edit, add and remove from a quick menu (Y), recent playlist.
- **Playback:** shuffle (X), repeat song, queue ("play next"), follow the current song, previous/next (L1/R1), seeking (L2/R2 and up/down), volume with indicator.
- **Song screen** with a spectrum visualizer and a mini panel while browsing.
- **Sleep:** timer, fade types, button lock, keep the screen on. Here sleep is handled by the service (pocket mode) and the interface **stops drawing entirely** (`SDL_WaitEvent`), instead of just lowering the FPS.
- **Settings:** color themes, interface scale, margins, CRT effect, 24 h clock, battery, network, effects volume, swap A/B and X/Y.
- **About** (in Settings): a tile with the author's GitHub; A opens a panel with its QR code (`qr.go`, with `skip2/go-qrcode`, pure Go), enlarged to a whole number of pixels per module so a phone can read it, and B closes it.

New compared to Songo: exiting while leaving the music playing, and entering pocket mode from anywhere.
